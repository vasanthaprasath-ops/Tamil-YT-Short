# 02 — பாண்டி தெருக்கள் ஏன் chess board மாதிரி?
Series: Why is Tamil Nadu like this? (City) | Status: film built (v8) | Date: 2026-11-13 | Target length: ~45 s

## Takeaway
Pondy's straight, chessboard streets are usually credited only to the French. But the grid first shows up on **Dutch** maps from 1693–94, when the Dutch briefly held the town. The French took it back and completed that plan. Later the French dug a canal through the middle: a storm-water drain that also split the town into a French side and a Tamil side.

## Sources
1. Origins of the Urban Development of Pondicherry according to Seventeenth Century Dutch Plans. French Institute of Pondicherry, via OpenEdition Books: https://books.openedition.org/ifp/5628
   - Dutch plans dated 1693–1694, held at the National Archives in The Hague, show the orthogonal street pattern. The French, back from about 1699–1700, carried the plan to completion. French historians dispute the Dutch credit.
2. Sahapedia, "The Grid and the Canal": https://www.sahapedia.org/grid-and-canal
   - Governor Jean Law de Lauriston rebuilt the town after 1765.
   - The canal was planned then and built slowly between 1770 and 1827.
   - It served as a storm-water drain carrying excess water to the sea, and as a divider: the European "White Town" by the sea, the Tamil town to the west.
3. Supporting: INTACH Pondicherry "Talking street" leaflet: https://intachpondicherry.org/flipbook/pdf/Talking_street_leaflet.pdf

**Fact limits:**
- The exact year of the French return (1699 vs 1700) differs between sources, so the script doesn't name it.
- Canal dates come mainly from heritage sources, so the VO avoids specific dates for the canal.
- The Dutch-vs-French credit is debated, so the script says the grid "first shows up" on Dutch maps, not that the Dutch "designed Pondy".
