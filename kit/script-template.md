# Script Template

Target: **30–45 seconds of measured voice-over** by default (go longer only when your recent Shorts hold a flat retention curve). The word count follows from your own measured narration pace; English words-per-second figures don't transfer to Tamil. Change the visual at **each new idea or sentence**, not on a timer, and keep every still moving. **Pace in seconds:** at most 3.6 s per sentence, one idea per sentence, and pauses over 0.25 s adding up to no more than 1 s per 40 s (one ≤ 0.5 s think-pause after the mystery question is fine). This structure comes from the research report (sections 4 and 5). It combines Zack D. Films' "thing happening in second one" opening, the curiosity-gap research, guess-before-reveal, and the Debunking Handbook's Fact → Myth → Fallacy → Fact order.

## Structure

| Time | Beat | What's on screen | What's said |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–1 s | **Thing happening** | The thing is *already* happening to Mani, **plus the Tamil question on screen from frame 0**. No intro, no title card, no black frame | Narration starts within the first second (the whoosh sits under it) |
| 0–3 s | **Surprise as a fact** | Tamil question in on-screen text | Sentence 1 **states** the surprise as a fact: the belief or fact plus the thing that breaks it. The spoken paradox lands by second 3 |
| 2–4 s | **Foreshadow** *(other series)* | Hint of the payoff image | One line saying what the viewer will get by the end. In the Paati series the guess beat does this job |
| 2–4 s | **Guess beat** *(Paati series only)* | Three stamps flash: உண்மை / பாதி உண்மை / பொய் | "Guess pannunga…" |
| ~10–50 % | **Escalation** | One visual per beat, each a bit bigger | At least 3 "but / therefore" beats; one is an exaggeration joke ("so slow that…") |
| ~45 % | **Mystery beat** | The viewer's guess, visualised | Exactly one question to the viewer before the reveal (the Paati guess beat counts) |
| ~50–55 % | **Reveal** | The surprising detail or close-up | The answer: what really happens |
| ~55–65 % | **Mechanism + analogy** | The everyday thing it is like | "ஏன்னா…" / "விஷயம் என்னன்னா…" + one everyday Tamil analogy (kitchen, kolam, bus, cricket, Paati's house) explaining *why* |
| last 2–3 s | **Twist / verdict** | Stamp lands, then the clean verdict card | The line people will repeat: the **fact**, said as the surprising or ironic twist (never end on the myth). No recap ("சுருக்கமா…"). The health 'see a doctor if…' line goes on the card and in the description, not as an extra spoken beat after the payoff. |
| end | **Hard cut** | Cut within ~0.5 s of the payoff or stamp landing | Nothing. No outro, recap or spoken subscribe ask. A loop line is optional and only if it adds no time |

*Changed 2026-11-14 (`kit/film-craft.md` §1):* the 1–3 s spoken question became a stated surprise (the reference channel opens with a fact in 20 of 20 videos; no data favours questions), and the escalation, mystery and mechanism + analogy beats were added. Percentages are shares of the runtime, measured on that channel.

**Body, object, survival and city series:** skip the guess beat and the stamp. End on the one-line takeaway card instead.

**Paati series (myth order):** state the true fact first. Mention the myth once ("Paati sollvaanga…"). Explain why people believed it (what she noticed). End on the fact. The stamp tells the viewer which way it went.

**Health rule:** never give "UNMAI" to a remedy that contradicts health-authority guidance. Name the authority on screen or in the description (WHO, ICMR, MoHFW, NHS). Body videos explain what is happening inside; they never prescribe a cure.

## Worked example (Body series): "Kaal maraththu pochu"

> **Facts to confirm before recording:** the NHS "pins and needles" page and NINDS "paresthesia" information. The explanation below is standard physiology, but re-check the wording against those pages so the narration doesn't overstate anything.

```
SERIES: Body, Tamil life
TITLE: கால் மரத்துப் போனா உள்ள என்ன நடக்குது? 😵 Nerve | Udambu
ONE-LINE TRUE TAKEAWAY: Long pressure squeezes the nerve and its blood supply, so the signals stop; when the pressure lifts, the nerve restarts with a burst of "pins and needles". It is usually harmless and goes away in minutes.
SOURCES: NHS – Pins and needles; NINDS – Paresthesia

[0–1s]  VISUAL: Mani stands up after a long sit on the floor at a wedding lunch; his leg buckles comically.
[1–3s]  ON-SCREEN: கால் மரத்துப் போனா உள்ள என்ன நடக்குது?
        VO: "Kaal maraththu pona, ulla enna nadakkudhu theriyuma?"
        FORESHADOW VO: "Kadaisila andha 'pins and needles'-ku kaaranam sollaren."
SHOT 3  VISUAL: X-ray cutaway of Mani's leg, a glowing nerve running down like a wire.
        VO: "Unga kaalla, moolaiku signal anuppura nerve-nu oru wire irukku."
SHOT 4  VISUAL: Mani's own weight pressing down on that wire; the glow dims.
        VO: "Romba neram oraye position-la ukkaandha, unga weight andha nerve-a amukkudhu."
SHOT 5  VISUAL: A tiny blood vessel beside the nerve narrowing.
        VO: "Kooda, nerve-ku blood supply-um kuraiyudhu."
SHOT 6  VISUAL: The signal stops halfway like a broken phone line; Mani taps his leg and feels nothing.
        VO: "Signal moolai varaikkum poga mudiyaadhu. Adhaan 'maraththu pochu'."
SHOT 7  VISUAL: Mani stands, the pressure lifts, and the nerve lights up with sparks.
        VO: "Ezhundha udane, nerve thirumba 'on' aagudhu…"
SHOT 8 (payoff) VISUAL: Close-up of hundreds of tiny sparks; Mani's face twists.
        VO: "…oru naalu nerathula ella signal-um sethu anuppudhu. Adhaan andha 'pins and needles' feeling!"
[end]   CARD: "Nerve amukkapattu signal nikkudhu. Free aana udane thirumba start aagudhu. Konja nerathula sari aayidum. Kaaranam illaama adikkadi maraththu pona, doctor-a paarunga."
        VO: "Konja neram nadandha sari aayidum."
        DESCRIPTION (add): "Kaaranam illaama adikkadi maraththu pona, doctor-a paarunga."
MEASURED VO LENGTH: __ s (target 30–45)
```

*Note (2026-11-14):* this example predates the fact-first hook. In a new script, sentence 1 would state the surprise as a fact instead of asking "…theriyuma?". Shot 6's "broken phone line" is already the everyday analogy the mechanism beat asks for; open it with "ஏன்னா…".

Why this works: the viewer has felt this exact thing (self-relevance). The first frame shows it happening. The answer is half-known (peak curiosity). The sparks are the visual payoff. The safety line points to a doctor instead of giving a treatment.
