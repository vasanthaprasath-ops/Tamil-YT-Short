# Win the first second, then stop

The craft of a high-performing faceless explainer Short rests on a surprisingly small evidence base, and that is itself the most useful finding: **only three things are well supported. The swipe decision in the first second, watch time held to the end, and simple, clear language.** YouTube measures the first-second decision directly ("Viewed vs. swiped away"), Instagram measures it as "skip rate" over the first 3 seconds, and the largest creator dataset (Paddy Galloway, 5,400 Shorts) found that Shorts under 60% viewed "rarely performed well" and that longer average view duration went with far more views. Almost everything else in the popular playbook, including hook "formulas", loop endings, cut-every-two-seconds rules, posting times, −14 LUFS, "85% watch on mute" and most viral percentages, is practitioner opinion at best and folklore at worst. For our Tamil channel that means four practical rules: make frame 0 name the subject with the sound off, foreshadow the payoff within about 3 seconds, build every middle line as a "but" or "therefore", and **cut the video within half a second of the answer landing**. The kit is mostly right. It needs five real corrections: loop lines become optional, the fixed 2–3 s cut rhythm becomes "one visual per idea", the 90–140 word target becomes a measured-seconds target of 30–45 s, the captions justification gets softened, and hook re-tests must never be done by re-uploading. No source anywhere studied Tamil or Indian-language Shorts, so every Tamil-specific rule below is an inference, and is labelled as one.

**How to read the grades.** Every claim carries one tag. **[OFFICIAL]** means the platform's own help page or a named platform employee (when relayed by press, it says so). **[PEER-REVIEWED]** means a published academic study. **[PRACTITIONER-DATA]** means a named creator or analyst with numbers. **[PRACTITIONER-OPINION]** means a named creator's advice without numbers. **[VENDOR]** means a tool seller or agency blog, often with a product to sell. **[MYTH/UNVERIFIED]** means a widely repeated claim with no traceable source. **[INFERENCE]** marks my own reasoning from the evidence, not a source.

## The first second is the only hook rule with hard evidence

The swipe decision is real and measured. YouTube Studio reports, for every Short, how many times it was "Shown in feed" and the percentage "Viewed (vs. swiped away)", plus a "Stayed to watch" metric for viewers who stay "past the initial seconds" **[OFFICIAL]** ([YouTube Help: reach](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314355?hl=en); [YouTube Help: content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281)). YouTube's Shorts lead Todd Sherman explained in 2023 that a new Short is tested on a "seed audience" and expanded or tapered based on whether viewers choose to watch rather than swipe **[OFFICIAL, via press]** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/25/youtube-demystifies-the-shorts-algorithm-views-and-answers-other-creator-questions/)). YouTube publishes **no target number**. The only published benchmark is Galloway's: across 33 channels and about 3.3B views, Shorts under **60% viewed** rarely did well, top performers mostly sat at 70–90%, and a high rate still did not guarantee success **[PRACTITIONER-DATA]** ([Galloway thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html)). Jenny Hoyos puts the average scroll-through rate near 70% and hers near 85% **[PRACTITIONER-DATA]** ([Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)). One peer-reviewed study of TikTok ads from a single advertiser found most viewers left within the first quarter of the video, which confirms early drop-off is real, though it says nothing about organic explainers **[PEER-REVIEWED]** ([Salminen et al., ACM Hypertext '24](https://jyx.jyu.fi/handle/123456789/98478)).

What does *not* have evidence is **which hook wording wins**. The largest dataset, a vendor's AI-sorted set of 121,820 hooks, found only about a **31% spread** between the best hook type ("social proof", 232 median views) and the worst ("authority", 177), and its own authors conclude a slow or weak opening matters more than the type you pick **[VENDOR]** ([quso.ai](https://quso.ai/research/best-hooks-for-short-form)). A second vendor study of 4,784 hooks from million-view videos found questions in only 15.4% of hooks and identical question rates in Shorts and long-form for channels making both, with *fewer* questions in the biggest (10M+) Shorts tier **[VENDOR]** ([OverseerOS](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/million-view-youtube-hooks-study)). So "always open with a question" and "always say *you*" are not rules. They are variables to test.

The rules that survive are about clarity, and they come from practitioners whose own numbers back them. Kallaway's version is to name **one clear subject**, plant **one question** in every viewer's head, and make the visual, spoken and on-screen text hooks all say the same thing, because a vague hook lets each viewer imagine a different subject and leave **[PRACTITIONER-OPINION]** ([Recapio summary](https://recapio.com/digest/how-to-create-a-killer-hook-impossible-to-skip-by-kallaway)). Hoyos adds that the hook must be **visual and understandable on mute**, must pass the test "would this work as a long-form title and thumbnail?", and should be followed immediately by a **two-line spoken foreshadow**, usually within 3 seconds, that tells the viewer what they will get by the end **[PRACTITIONER-OPINION]** ([Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)). Galloway says to treat the intro "like a thumbnail" **[PRACTITIONER-DATA]**. The one verifiable teardown of our reference channel, Zack D. Films' "How Stitches Work" (31 s, about 253M views), shows the needle already at the skin in the first second and the question stated immediately **[PRACTITIONER-OPINION]** ([Rusnok](https://danielrusnok.substack.com/p/how-a-faceless-channel-engineered)).

For a Tamil channel this matters more than for an English one **[INFERENCE]**. Many viewers in the feed scroll with sound off, and some will not speak Tamil, so spoken Tamil cannot carry the first second. Frame 0 plus the on-screen Tamil question must name the subject by themselves. The kit already shows "the thing happening" in second one. The gap is that the template leaves the first second silent and puts the on-screen question at 1–3 s, while the style sheet says 0–3 s. Put the question on screen from frame 0, and start the narration within the first second.

## The middle earns watch time by changing the situation every line

The second measured lever is how long people stay. Galloway found a "clear relationship" between longer Shorts and more views, and Shorts with **average view duration above 50 s averaged 4.1M views**, but he calls this early, correlational research from the 60-second era **[PRACTITIONER-DATA]** ([Galloway thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html)). He also found **no strong relationship** between likes, comments or shares and performance in his sample. Watch duration was what tracked views. YouTube's position, relayed secondhand, is that there is no ideal length and "the story sets it" **[OFFICIAL, via press]** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/25/youtube-demystifies-the-shorts-algorithm-views-and-answers-other-creator-questions/); [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)). Hoyos's most popular YouTube Shorts run about **34 s**, she says Shorts under 30 s need retention above 100% to take off, and on TikTok she found 10–20 s worked better **[PRACTITIONER-DATA]** ([Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)).

These findings pull in opposite directions, and the resolution is a trade-off rather than a number. Longer Shorts earn more total watch time *if* they hold, but completion falls as length rises (one vendor dataset puts TikTok completion at 92% under 15 s and 68% at 31–60 s **[VENDOR]**, method unchecked). Galloway's "longer is better" comes from established channels. A new Tamil channel with no proven retention should default to **30–45 s** and go longer only when its own retention graph stays flat **[INFERENCE]**. That is narrower than the kit's 30–60 s, not a contradiction of it.

How to keep people in the middle has no isolated data at all, only consistent practitioner craft. Hoyos builds every story with **"but/therefore"** links, the Trey Parker and Matt Stone rule that each beat must change the situation rather than add "and then" **[PRACTITIONER-OPINION]** ([Perell](https://perell.com/note/but-therefore-rule/)). She gives viewers a **visible mechanism**, such as three steps, so they know how close the payoff is, and she sets an expectation and then twists it **[PRACTITIONER-OPINION]** ([Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)). Brendan Kane warns that hooks that do not deliver "train audiences to disengage" **[PRACTITIONER-OPINION]** ([Summaries.com](https://www.summaries.com/blog/hook-point)). For our channel, "but/therefore" is already the shape of the Paati verdict arc: the belief, *but* the evidence, *therefore* the real answer. The Paati guess beat (three stamps flashing) is the visible mechanism and can double as the foreshadow **[INFERENCE]**. Specific timings like "re-hook every 7 seconds" appear only in vendor blogs and should be ignored. The rule is "every line changes the situation", and the retention graph is the check.

Language economy has one quantified rule. Hoyos writes at a **5th-grade reading level or lower**, checked with a readability tool. Top Shorts she scraped, including MrBeast's, scored about 1st grade, and a single jargon word can push a script from grade 5 to grade 8, so she defines terms instead **[PRACTITIONER-DATA]** ([Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)). Readability tools are English-only. The Tamil equivalent is spoken colloquial Tamil, short clauses, everyday words instead of formal written Tamil, and every technical term explained in one breath **[INFERENCE]**. No source gives a retention-optimal speaking speed. The "2.5 words per second, 80 words per 30 s" rule of thumb is a vendor figure for English **[VENDOR, MYTH/UNVERIFIED]** ([OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-script-template/)), and Tamil words are longer and agglutinative, so word counts do not transfer. Measure your own narration in seconds and let that set the script length.

## End at the answer; loops and subscribe asks are unproven

"End right after the payoff" is the best-supported ending rule. Hoyos says "whatever you say you're gonna do, you end it right after you do it". Trimming **one second** from one ending moved retention from **83% to 88%**, and the video took off. It is a single anecdote, but it is a measured one **[PRACTITIONER-DATA, n=1]** ([Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)). Zack D. Films' Stitches Short ends "where curiosity ends", with **no loop, no call to action and an empty description** **[PRACTITIONER-OPINION]** ([Rusnok](https://danielrusnok.substack.com/p/how-a-faceless-channel-engineered)). Hoyos also writes the last line before the middle **[PRACTITIONER-OPINION]**.

Loop endings, where the last line flows back into the first frame, are **not evidenced**. No YouTube statement says loops boost distribution, and the claim that replays are a top-weighted signal appears only in unattributed vendor blogs **[MYTH/UNVERIFIED]** ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/double-youtube-watch-time-looping-shorts)). Since 31 March 2025 YouTube counts a public Shorts view on every start *and replay*, while the old threshold lives on as **"engaged views"**, which exclude loops and which govern Partner Programme eligibility and revenue **[OFFICIAL, via press]** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted); [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281)). A loop can pad the public counter and push average % viewed above 100%, but there is no proof it raises reach and it does nothing for the engaged views our money path depends on. Keep a loop line only when it costs no time and does not blur the payoff. Never trade a clean ending for one.

Spoken "subscribe" asks have no controlled evidence either way. Galloway's data shows Shorts convert fewer subscribers per view than long-form, **16.9 vs 22.7 per 10,000 views**, with the gap reversing for 1M+ subscriber channels, which he *guesses* is due to better Shorts and better calls to action **[PRACTITIONER-DATA]** ([The Publish Press](https://news.thepublishpress.com/p/are-shorts-worth-it)). Creators report that spoken calls to action show as dips on the retention graph, but a vendor points out the dip may just mean the value was already delivered **[PRACTITIONER-OPINION; VENDOR]** ([ContentStudio](https://contentstudio.io/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics-guide)). Our model channel uses none. The default is **no spoken subscribe ask**. Earn the follow with recurring identity instead: the verdict stamp, Paati and the series name **[INFERENCE]**.

One real conflict with the kit is the health-safety line. The worked example ends with a spoken "see a doctor if…" line and then a loop line, both after the payoff. The safety line is a policy requirement and outranks a few points of retention. Put it on the takeaway card and in the description, and keep it as short as possible in the narration, while the loop line goes **[INFERENCE]**.

## Captions, voice, sound and pacing: the kit is mostly right for the wrong reasons

**Captions.** The famous "85% of video is watched without sound" comes from a 2016 Digiday piece quoting a few Facebook publishers. It is not a platform measurement and predates Shorts and Reels **[MYTH/UNVERIFIED]** ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/silent-world-facebook-video/)). TikTok's "88%" is a TikTok-commissioned survey saying sound is "essential", not a sound-on share **[VENDOR]** ([TikTok for Business](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/kantar-report-how-brands-are-making-noise-and-driving-impact-with-sound-on-tiktok)). Captions are still right, for better reasons. Gernsbacher's review of 100+ studies found captions improve comprehension, attention and memory, with the **largest gains for second-language viewers** and early readers **[PEER-REVIEWED]** ([Gernsbacher 2015](https://successforkidswithhearingloss.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/Video-Captions-Benefit-Everyone_2015.pdf)). A critic found no objective studies for typical learners reading same-language captions and argues captions can overload viewers on complex material **[PRACTITIONER-OPINION]** ([Learning and the Brain](https://www.learningandthebrain.com/blog/video-captions-benefit-everyone-an-investigation/)), so the kit's "for everyone" overstates it. Faster subtitles reduce comprehension **[PEER-REVIEWED]** ([Applied Psycholinguistics](https://www-cambridge-org.ezproxyberklee.flo.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/A7DB0CBF5910353143738CF9C1721317/S0142716421000503a.pdf/why-subtitle-speed-matters-evidence-from-word-skipping-and-rereading.pdf)), and nothing tests the trendy word-by-word "kinetic" captions. For long Tamil words, show **short phrases of 2–4 words timed to speech**, not single flashing words **[INFERENCE]**. One turmeric keyword highlight per line is untested and free to A/B **[PRACTITIONER-OPINION]**.

**Voice-over.** No study links speaking speed to Shorts retention. A UCLA study found comprehension barely changed between 1x and 2x playback (26/40 vs 25/40 on the immediate test), but older adults were hurt more by fast speech and prefer slower delivery **[PEER-REVIEWED]** ([ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2022/01/220111153637.htm); [Castel lab](https://castel.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/111/2023/04/The-effect-of-video-playback-speed-on-learning-and-mind-wandering-in-younger-and-older-adults.pdf)). The risk for our channel is jargon and the Paati-age audience, not speed **[INFERENCE]**. Cut words, not tempo. AI-versus-human voice studies are mixed and mostly vendor-funded. Listeners often cannot tell the difference in blind tests, but they say they trust humans more (77% in an Adobe survey), and opinions shift once listeners learn a voice is AI **[VENDOR]** ([ContentGrip](https://www.contentgrip.com/ai-voices-in-marketing-adobe/); [Radio Ink](https://radioink.com/2026/07/07/radio-listeners-cant-detect-ai-voice-but-dont-trust-it-either/)). YouTube's policy targets "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates", not text-to-speech as such **[OFFICIAL]** ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)). The kit's own-voice decision stands. The −14 LUFS loudness target is widely repeated, but no YouTube page states it **[MYTH/UNVERIFIED as official; PRACTITIONER-OPINION as practice]** ([Production Advice](https://productionadvice.co.uk/youtube-loudness/)). The kit already labels it "common guidance", which is correct.

**Sound.** There is no retention data on whooshes, risers or silence. The only audio numbers are TikTok-commissioned ad attitudes **[VENDOR]**. The firm ground is licensing: YouTube Audio Library tracks "won't be claimed", and Shorts over one minute with *any* Content ID claim are **blocked globally** **[OFFICIAL]** ([YouTube Help 15424877](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en)). This confirms the kit's no-licensed-music rule. A fixed verdict sting as an audio brand cue is reasonable sonic-branding opinion **[PRACTITIONER-OPINION]**. Keep it under 1 second and never before the hook, because Galloway's swipe data punishes a slow first second **[INFERENCE]**. If you use a music bed, keep it far below the voice (roughly −25 to −30 LUFS under a −14 LUFS voice-over), because YouTube's mobile "Stable Volume" compresses the mix **[PRACTITIONER-OPINION, no source fetched]**.

**Visual pacing.** No public frame-by-frame cut count of Zack D. Films exists, and "cut every 1–3 seconds" rules are folklore **[MYTH/UNVERIFIED]**. What is documented is a question in the first frame, constant motion, and an end at the answer, at a production cost of about **$1,000 per 20–45 s clip** **[PRACTITIONER-DATA, job listing]** ([Quickdraw Animation](https://quickdrawanimation.ca/opportunities/job-postings/zack-d-films-animator-wanted)). Galloway's and Hoyos's data reward *removing dead time*, not adding cuts. Change the visual at each new idea or sentence, and keep every still moving with a push-in or parallax. The kit already does the second part.

**Packaging.** No official source says Shorts titles drive feed distribution. Practitioners agree titles matter mostly for search and the channel page, and one analysis found no advantage from putting "#Shorts" in the title **[PRACTITIONER-OPINION; PRACTITIONER-DATA, unverified]** ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-titles/); [OverseerOS](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/should-you-put-shorts-in-youtube-title)). YouTube said in 2023 that custom thumbnails are unnecessary in the swipe feed and matter only on shelves and entry points **[OFFICIAL, via press]** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/25/youtube-demystifies-the-shorts-algorithm-views-and-answers-other-creator-questions/)). So the **first frame is the real thumbnail**. Design frame 0 as the cover (Tamil question inside the safe box, Mani mid-reaction, no black or fade-in frame), select it as the Shorts thumbnail, and use it as the Reels cover. Instagram's profile grid crops covers to the centre 3:4 (about 1080×1440) **[VENDOR]** ([Planoly](https://planoly.com/blog/guide-to-instagrams-new-vertical-grid)), and the kit's safe box (y 260–1470) already sits inside that crop. Zack D. Films' one-emoji titles are a cheap habit to copy, not an evidence-backed lever.

## What the three platforms officially reward and punish

**YouTube Shorts.** The official picture is that a Short is tested on a seed audience and expanded if people choose to watch. There is no ideal length. Posting time does not matter except for news. Upload volume does not drive traction ("quality over quantity"). **Deleting and re-uploading** a Short to revive it may be treated as spam **[OFFICIAL, via press, 2023]** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/25/youtube-demystifies-the-shorts-algorithm-views-and-answers-other-creator-questions/)). In September 2026 YouTube's Todd Beaupré reportedly said no single metric drives ranking. Clicks, absolute and relative watch time, likes, comments and survey satisfaction all count, with weights varying by viewer **[OFFICIAL, secondhand, no transcript]** ([ppc.land](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)). Shorts can now run to 3 minutes **[OFFICIAL]** ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en)), but the Help page is silent on how longer Shorts are ranked. Many of these statements date from 2023, before 3-minute Shorts, so treat them as the best available official picture, not the current algorithm.

**Instagram Reels.** Adam Mosseri named **watch time, likes and sends** as the top three signals in January 2025. He told creators to watch average watch time, likes per reach and **sends per reach**, and said sends matter slightly more for reaching non-followers **[OFFICIAL, via press]** ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/)). Comments were not on that list. Since April 2026, Reels insights show a retention chart, **skip rate** (the share of views skipped within the first 3 seconds) and share rate **[VENDOR/press describing product]** ([Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/news-2/instagram-retention-chart-skip-rate-new-performance-metrics-reels-9730992)). Instagram stops recommending Reels from accounts that mostly post content they did not create or "meaningfully transform" **[OFFICIAL, via press]** ([TubeFilter](https://tubefilter.com/2026/04/30/instagram-removes-algorithm-recommendations-repost-content-aggregator)). Reels carrying **other apps' watermarks** are made less discoverable, while your own logo is fine **[OFFICIAL, via press]** ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-clarifies-including-your-own-logo-on-a-reel-is-ok/730852)). **Trial Reels** show a Reel to non-followers first, which makes them the only shipped first-party hook test **[OFFICIAL]** ([Instagram Creators](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/instagram-trial-reels)).

**Facebook Reels.** No official Facebook Reels ranking-signal list was found. What Meta has stated is that since July 2025 accounts that repeatedly repost others' content lose distribution and can be demonetised **[OFFICIAL, via press]** ([iPhone in Canada](https://www.iphoneincanada.ca/2025/07/15/meta-crackdown-unoriginal-facebook-content/)). Meta also officially demotes **engagement bait**, posts that "explicitly request engagement" such as votes, shares, comments, tags or reactions, with bigger drops for Pages that repeat it **[OFFICIAL]** ([Meta Transparency Center](https://transparency.meta.com/features/approach-to-ranking/content-distribution-guidelines/engagement-bait/)).

**Engagement without bait.** Taken together, the official evidence says to design for watch time and **sends**, not comments. The kit's pinned question "Unga paati idhukku enna solluvaanga?" invites a genuine answer and falls outside Meta's definition. "Comment UNMAI if you agree" asks for a meaningless token and fits it, so never use that form **[INFERENCE from OFFICIAL]**. The verdict card built to be forwarded to a family WhatsApp group is exactly the "send" Mosseri describes. Replying to comments in the first hour is good community practice, but no source shows it lifts reach.

**Originality and series.** YouTube's July 2025 "inauthentic content" policy targets mass-produced, templated videos, such as narrated stories with only superficial differences, and examines titles and thumbnails too. AI that supports original characters, scripts or visuals is allowed **[OFFICIAL]** ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)). A fixed intro, stamp and mascot are fine, but each episode needs a distinct script, distinct visuals and a distinct title, and you should never reuse narration or near-identical animation across episodes **[INFERENCE]**.

## Series help packaging, but measurement decides what survives

YouTube made series a product feature in September 2026. "Shorts series" groups Shorts into seasons and episodes with sequential playback **[OFFICIAL, via press]** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/23/youtubes-new-short-series-feature-brings-episodic-viewing-to-shorts/)). Separately, a "related video" link can point each Short at another video on your channel **[OFFICIAL]** ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14075157?hl=en)), though one creator analysis says it sends only a few hundred viewers per 100,000 Short views **[PRACTITIONER-DATA, unverified]**. No source has data showing that series raise follow rate, and the biggest faceless explainer channel, Zack D. Films (about 27.8M subscribers), has **no named series** at all. Its identity is a consistent style and a consistent question shape **[PRACTITIONER-DATA]** ([CreatorDB](https://creatordb.app/creatorstats/zackdfilms/)). Series are a cheap add-on, not a requirement. The kit's plan (run three series, judge each after four or more episodes) stands. The additions are to register each surviving series as a Shorts series in Studio and to point the related-video link at the series' best performer.

The measure-and-iterate loop works only on relative numbers, because **no platform publishes "good" thresholds** for any of its metrics. Benchmarks such as "aim for 75–80% viewed" and "76% average retention for viral Shorts" trace to vendor blogs **[VENDOR, MYTH/UNVERIFIED]** ([ContentStudio](https://contentstudio.io/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics-guide)). Read each Short in three steps. First, did they stop (YouTube Viewed%, Instagram skip rate)? Second, did they stay (where the retention curve drops)? Third, did they pass it on (sends or shares per reach)? Compare each against your own median, and use engaged views, not public views, for period comparisons **[OFFICIAL, via press]** ([ppc.land](https://ppc.land/youtube-changes-how-shorts-views-are-counted-from-march-31/)). Galloway's 60% floor is a useful red flag for a weak first second.

On testing hooks, YouTube's thumbnail Test & Compare **does not work on Shorts** **[OFFICIAL]** ([YouTube Help 13861714](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714)). A feature that tests up to three different video cuts, aimed at testing hooks, was announced in September 2026 as "coming soon" **[OFFICIAL, announced, not shipped]** ([ppc.land](https://ppc.land/youtube-to-test-up-to-3-versions-of-one-video-on-real-viewers-shorts-too/)). Until it appears in your Studio, **Trial Reels are the only first-party hook test**. Re-uploading a near-identical Short with a new hook risks both YouTube's spam warning and the inauthentic-content policy **[INFERENCE from OFFICIAL]**. The project report already says to re-test via Trial Reels, and the checklist should say it too.

Real teardowns remain a gap. Only the Zack D. Films Stitches Short has a verifiable public breakdown, and even that lacks a beat-by-beat map. No credible breakdowns were found for Kurzgesagt, Infographics Show, FactTechz, Madan Gowri or Tamil channels. The honest fix is to do 5–8 yourself. Pull auto-subtitles and metadata with yt-dlp (titles and text only, never re-uploading anyone's video), then log for each: the first frame, the words before the question mark, cuts per 5 s, the payoff timestamp as a percentage of length, and the ending type **[INFERENCE]**.

## Per-Short checklist (one page, use before every Short)

**Before writing**
- [ ] One subject, one question. The one-line true takeaway is written down, with two credible sources.
- [ ] Write the **hook and the last line first**, then the middle. [PRACTITIONER-OPINION]

**Hook (0–3 s)**
- [ ] **Frame 0** shows the thing already happening, with the Tamil question on screen from the first frame. No black frame, fade-in, logo or sting before it. [PRACTITIONER-DATA: Galloway swipe floor]
- [ ] **Mute test:** frame 0 plus the on-screen text alone tell a non-Tamil, sound-off viewer what this is about. [PRACTITIONER-OPINION]
- [ ] Visual, spoken and text hooks say the **same thing**. [PRACTITIONER-OPINION]
- [ ] Narration starts within the first second.
- [ ] A **foreshadow line** by about 3 s tells viewers what they get by the end (Paati: the guess beat does this). [PRACTITIONER-OPINION]

**Body**
- [ ] Every line is a **"but"** or a **"therefore"**. Cut every "and then". [PRACTITIONER-OPINION]
- [ ] Spoken colloquial Tamil, short clauses, every technical word explained in one breath or cut. [INFERENCE from PRACTITIONER-DATA]
- [ ] A new visual at **each new idea**, and no still sits still (push-in or parallax). [INFERENCE]
- [ ] A twist on the expected answer. [PRACTITIONER-OPINION]

**Ending**
- [ ] The video **cuts within about 0.5 s of the payoff landing**. No outro, recap or spoken subscribe ask. [PRACTITIONER-DATA, n=1]
- [ ] The verdict card is readable as a screenshot. The health "see a doctor if…" line is on the card and in the description.

## Where this confirms or contradicts the existing research and kit

The project's research and kit hold up well, mostly because they were already built on the same small set of sources. These parts are **confirmed**:

- The first-second "thing happening" opening, burned-in Tamil captions inside the safe box, own-voice narration, no licensed music, and clean native uploads to each platform.
- Writing the takeaway first, honest verdicts, and pinned questions that ask for real information.
- Judging a series only after four or more episodes, comparing against the channel's own median, and avoiding engagement-bait comments.
- PROJECT-CONTEXT §5's note that viral "first 3 seconds" percentages have no source, and §8's claim that YouTube does not reward daily posting.
- The project report's §7 instruction to re-test hooks through Trial Reels.
- The safe box sitting inside Instagram's 3:4 grid crop.

These parts are **contradicted or need softening**:

- **Loops:** the template's loop-line row and the checklist's "loop line flows back" item treat an unproven lever as required.
- **Cut rate:** "a new visual every 2–3 s" is a timer rule the evidence does not support.
- **Length:** "90–140 words" imports a word-count logic that doesn't transfer to Tamil, and 30–60 s is wider than a new channel should default to.
- **Captions:** the style sheet's "for everyone" overstates Gernsbacher.
- **Character claims:** the style sheet's "recurring characters drive follows" claims more than any data shows.
- **The old scriptwriting notes:** Galloway's length finding is misquoted, and the source behind it (Bytecap) could not be verified.
- **Hook re-tests:** checklist §E's "rewrite the hook and re-test" says nothing about *how*. The only safe ways are Trial Reels or YouTube's cut-test feature once it ships, never a YouTube re-upload.

## Conclusion

The striking result is how little of the short-form playbook is knowledge rather than habit. Once the folklore is stripped out, the evidence reduces to one measured gate (stop the swipe), one measured outcome (hold to the end), and a set of practitioner craft rules that all serve those two numbers. That is good news for a beginner. It means the right investment is not memorising hook formulas or cut rates. It is getting frame 0, the foreshadow line and the hard ending right, then letting your own analytics, read against your own median, tell you what works for Tamil viewers. No outside study can tell you that, because none exists.

The biggest open risk is not craft but testing. YouTube's video-cut A/B test is announced but not confirmed live, and the tempting shortcut of re-uploading a Short with a new hook collides with both the spam guidance and the inauthentic-content policy. Until the official tool appears in Studio, the channel's learning loop runs through Trial Reels on Instagram and through *next* episodes on YouTube. Each Short should be treated as one data point in a series experiment, not something to rescue. The second open gap is Tamil itself. Caption reading speed, narration pace and hook behaviour have never been studied for Tamil, so the first 20 Shorts are the research. Log them as carefully as the scripts.
