# Rent Two AI Tools, Free-Ride the Rest

Vasanth needs only two paid AI services to start. The first is a rupee-billed Google AI plan, for consistent Mani and Paati stills (and Veo video if needed). The second is ElevenLabs Starter, for the Paati voice changer and English/Hindi dubs. Everything else can be free: DaVinci Resolve on Windows, YouTube Studio's Tamil auto-captions for timing, Google Fonts Tamil families, Adobe Podcast Enhance for clean-up, and CC0 or self-made sound effects. The **LEAN stack costs ₹399 a month** if Google's free Omni Flash video tool in YouTube Create accepts his own still. That is unverified in India. If it doesn't, the stack costs **₹1,950**, and both cases add **₹645** once Paati lines or dubs begin. The **STANDARD stack adds Kling Pro** for character animation and comes to about **₹6,100 a month** (₹88/$ plus 3.5% card forex plus 18% GST). One-time spend is a **₹2,000–2,600 USB mic**. Money is not the binding constraint; time is. A 45-second Short realistically takes **3.3–4.5 hours once templates exist and 5–6.5 hours for the first ten**, so the earlier plan of 4–5 Shorts a week does not fit 12–15 free hours. Start at **2 a week** and earn each step up with logged times. Three facts reshape the earlier plan. **CapCut is blocked in India** (since late July 2020, not the June list). **Sora is shut down.** **No image model reliably renders Tamil script**, so all Tamil text goes on in the editor and must be checked in an exported frame. Clay-style animation needs no AI disclosure. Google-made clips carry a permanent label, which for animated content should sit in the description, and labels do not affect monetisation. None of these tools has been tested on Tamil speech, on a clay character, or on Vasanth's hardware. A bake-off day costing about ₹110 settles the final picks.

**How to read this report.** Sourced facts carry an inline link. **(inference)** marks my judgement built on that evidence. **(unverified)** marks a claim from a single secondary source, a conflict between sources, or something not checked in this research. Every price carries the date its source was checked. Nothing here was tested hands-on.

**Currency assumption, used for every USD figure.** USD prices are converted at **₹88/$**. This rate is an assumption: no November 2026 rate was fetched. Add a 3.5% card forex markup and 18% GST, using the formula `P × rate × 1.035 × 1.18` from an Indian developer's cost breakdown ([dev.to](https://dev.to/arjitjindal/what-your-dev-stack-actually-costs-in-india-after-forex-gst-3oe3)). That gives **₹107.5 per $1**. At that article's own rate of ₹95.6, the multiplier is **₹116.8**. If a vendor charges no GST, only GST on the markup applies and the multiplier drops to about **₹91.6**. Few foreign tools charge GST: one author found that only 8 of 120 issued a GST invoice ([dev.to](https://dev.to/arjitjindal/i-checked-120-foreign-software-tools-for-a-gst-invoice-eight-had-one-2ccn)). The ₹107.5 figure is therefore a ceiling at the assumed rate. An individual cannot reclaim the GST ([StartupTalky](https://startuptalky.com/getting-wrong-gst-chatgpt-claude-foreign-ai-tools/)). Google and OpenAI bill in rupees in India. ChatGPT Plus's ₹1,999 includes GST ([kingy.ai](https://kingy.ai/blog/chatgpt-claude-gemini-prices-india/)). Whether Google's ₹ prices include GST was not confirmed.

## Your voice stays human; AI only bends Paati and translates

**Own narration is both the cheapest and the safest voice.** YouTube's monetisation policy names "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates" as non-monetisable. It treats original voiceover or commentary as what makes content acceptable, and it never mentions text-to-speech ([YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)). TTS therefore stays a sick-day fallback. For that fallback, Sarvam's Bulbul v3 is Indian-built, supports Tamil and costs **₹30 per 10,000 characters** (beta pricing, accessed 2026-11-04) ([Sarvam pricing](https://docs.sarvam.ai/api-reference-docs/pricing)). ElevenLabs v3 and Multilingual v2 also support Tamil ([ElevenLabs models](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/models)). No source compared Tamil naturalness head to head, so the two would need a side-by-side listen on one script **(inference)**.

**A ₹2,000–2,600 USB condenser mic is the whole hardware budget.** The Fifine K669B was about ₹1,999 in one May 2026 list and ₹2,300–2,600 in another. It has no official India warranty, so Amazon's return window is the only protection ([earlyviewnewstipper, May 2026](https://earlyviewnewstipper.blogspot.com/2026/05/best-budget-microphones-under-3000-in.html); [getpc.co.in](https://getpc.co.in/parts/usb-microphones/fifine-k669b)). The Maono AU-A04 lists at ₹2,499–3,499 ([getpc.co.in](https://getpc.co.in/parts/usb-microphones/maono-au-a04)). For phone recording, the wired Boya BY-M1 lavalier was **₹848 on Amazon.in on 6 July 2026** ([MagicDrop](https://magicdrop.in/drops/boya-mic-price)). A phone without a 3.5 mm jack needs a USB-C dongle that supports mic input **(inference)**. Wireless lavaliers such as the Hollyland Lark M2 cost ₹11,990–13,490 at dealers ([Videolinks](https://videolinks.com/products/hollyland-lark-m2-combo)). They solve a walk-and-talk-to-camera problem that a faceless channel doesn't have. All mic prices are blog or dealer snapshots, so check Amazon.in before buying.

**Clean-up is free.** Adobe Podcast Enhance Speech's free tier is reported at about **1 hour of audio per day** with 30-minute files ([gauravtiwari.org](https://gauravtiwari.org/adobe-podcast-review/); [aitoolsatlas](https://aitoolsatlas.ai/tools/adobe-podcast/free-vs-paid)). That is far more than a few one-minute voiceovers a week, though Adobe's own limits page was not opened. DaVinci Resolve's AI Voice Isolation is probably a paid Studio feature: one source reads the July 2026 Resolve 21 manual that way, another describes it in Resolve 20 free. The Dialogue Leveler is free either way ([DaVinci Resolve Club](https://davinciresolveclub.com/voice-isolation-vs-dialogue-leveler/); [Larry Jordan](https://larryjordan.com/articles/compare-ai-voice-isolation-ai-dialogue-leveler-ai-dialogue-separator-in-davinci-resolve-20/)). Master to about **−14 LUFS integrated with a −1 dBTP true-peak ceiling**. That is common practice, not an official YouTube number, and YouTube turns loud audio down but never boosts quiet audio ([Youlean](https://youlean.co/?p=30813); [Audacity forum](https://forum.audacityteam.org/t/how-to-get-maximum-loudness-for-youtube-on-voice-recording/58497)).

**ElevenLabs is the only voice changer with documented Tamil support, and only its paid plans allow monetised use.**

- **Tamil support:** its speech-to-speech model `eleven_multilingual_sts_v2` shares the 29-language set of Multilingual v2, which includes Tamil. It is built to keep the input performance: whispers, laughs, accent and emotional cues ([ElevenLabs models](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/models); [Voice Changer docs](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/capabilities/voice-changer)).
- **Cost:** conversion uses **1,000 credits per minute**, so a 10-second Paati line takes about 170 of the Starter plan's 30,000 monthly credits. Starter is **$6/month, $1 for the first month** (accessed 2026-11-04) ([ElevenLabs pricing](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing)).
- **Licence:** the free plan "does not include a commercial license" and requires "elevenlabs.io" in the video title. Output from beta features cannot be used commercially even on paid plans ([ElevenLabs help](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/help-center/legal/can-i-publish-the-content-i-generate-on-the-platform)).
- **Untested:** nobody has published a test of any tool on Tamil retroflex consonants or on male-to-elderly-female conversion, so Vasanth's ear decides.

The alternatives are weaker:

- **Kits.ai:** its free tier has **zero download minutes** ([Kits.ai pricing](https://kits.ai/pricing)).
- **Voicemod:** its commercial terms were not found.
- **RVC/Applio:** free and offline ([Applio docs](https://applio-docs.pages.dev/getting-started/inference)), but it needs a local GPU and a voice model. Community models are often trained on real people's voices without consent **(inference)**. One user reported RVC training freezing a PC with a 4 GB card ([goodfirstissue](https://www.goodfirstissue.org/RVC-Project/Retrieval-based-Voice-Conversion-WebUI/issues/1711)).

The real backup uses no AI at all: the narrator quotes Paati, as the channel's style sheet already does. Never imitate a real person's voice. ElevenLabs creates Professional Voice Clones only of the account holder's own verified voice ([ElevenLabs help](https://help.elevenlabs.io/hc/en-us/articles/36842751624209-Can-I-create-a-Professional-Voice-Clone-of-someone-else-s-voice)).

**Dubs have to come from ElevenLabs, because YouTube's auto-dub can't do the job.** YouTube's auto-dubbing supports Tamil only as **Tamil→English** (and English→Tamil). There is **no Tamil→Hindi pair**, and the Tamil→English pair lacks the "expressive speech" voice. Dubs cannot be edited, and the help page never says whether Shorts qualify ([YouTube Help 15569972](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15569972?hl=en)). Uploading your own audio tracks is the reliable route. YouTube's multi-language audio accepts tracks "to a single video or Short" ([YouTube Help 13338784](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13338784?hl=en)). It opened to all creators in September 2025, and YouTube reported that over 25% of watch time on videos using it came from non-primary languages ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/10/youtubes-multi-language-audio-feature-for-dubbing-videos-rolls-out-to-all-creators/)).

ElevenLabs Dubbing supports Tamil and Hindi and preserves speaker identity. Only free-tier dubs are watermarked ([ElevenLabs Dubbing](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/capabilities/dubbing)), and Instant Voice Cloning starts at Starter ([ElevenLabs pricing](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing)). Cloning your own voice for voiceovers or dubs needs **no YouTube disclosure** ([YouTube Help 14328491](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en)). On Instagram and Facebook, Meta's free AI translation now includes Tamil, keeps the creator's own voice, and labels the Reel "Translated with Meta AI" ([inro.social](https://www.inro.social/blog/meta-ai-reel-translation); [Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/news-2/instagram-facebook-ai-reel-translations-more-indian-languages-11017659)). It is a zero-cost way to test whether English or Hindi demand exists before paying for dubs **(inference)**. How many credits one 45-second dub uses is not clearly published, so measure it on bake-off day.

## Character stills cost ₹399 a month but need a human eye on every frame

**Google's Nano Banana family leads most 2026 consistency comparisons, but no independent benchmark exists.** A September 2026 test guide names the field: Nano Banana 2 (Gemini 3.1 Flash Image), GPT Image 2.5, Qwen-Image-2.1, Seedream 5.0 Lite and FLUX.2 [pro] ([Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/consistent-ai-character-generator-test-2026/)). Listicles rank Nano Banana first for multi-reference consistency ([renderreport](https://renderreport.com/blog/best-ai-image-generators-character-consistency)).

The only scored benchmark (140 images) has a conflict of interest: it was run by a vendor whose own product came first, on old model versions. It still documents the failure modes that matter ([ToonyStory](https://toonystory.com/blog/best-ai-for-character-consistency-2026)):

- Midjourney's character reference shifted **eye shape and skin tone** between scenes.
- Outfits drifted even at maximum reference weight.
- In multi-character scenes, most tools **swapped hair and outfits between characters**.

Every model does worse with two characters in a frame, and back views are where most fail. Outputs are non-deterministic, so each prompt needs at least two runs ([Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/consistent-ai-character-generator-test-2026/)). A 2026 preprint found Gemini and GPT image models skew toward lighter skin on neutral prompts, and prompt constraints only modestly fix it ([arXiv 2602.12133](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12133v1)). For Mani and Paati this means **(inference)**:

- Write skin tone into every prompt.
- Use single-character frames by default.
- Check every two-shot for Mani's mustard bleeding into Paati's saree.

**In India the cheapest serious route is a rupee-billed Google plan.** Google's India plans page (fetched 2026-11-04) lists four tiers ([Google AI plans India](https://gemini.google/in/subscriptions/?hl=en-IN)):

| Plan | Price | What it includes |
|---|---|---|
| Free | ₹0 | "Limited access to Nano Banana Pro" |
| AI Plus | ₹399/month | "More access to Nano Banana", 200 Flow credits |
| AI Pro | ₹1,950/month | 1,000 Flow credits |
| AI Ultra | from ₹6,500/month | 5× Pro limits |

Limits are stated only as multiples of Free (Plus 2×, Pro 4×). They refresh every five hours up to a weekly cap, and **no image count is published** for any plan. Two promotions may matter, though whether either still runs is unknown. AI Plus launched in December 2025 at ₹199 for six months for new users, and Jio users on qualifying plans got AI Pro free for 18 months ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/10/google-launches-sub-5-ai-plus-plan-in-india-to-compete-with-chatgpt-go)).

The other options:

- **ChatGPT Go:** ₹399/month with 10× Free's image generations, and it **accepts UPI** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/18/openai-launches-a-sub-5-chatgpt-plan-in-india)). OpenAI publishes no image counts either ([toolcolumn](https://www.toolcolumn.com/learn/chatgpt-image-generation-limits-by-plan)). Indian users who signed up during a 2025 promotion got Go free for a year ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/27/openai-offers-free-chatgpt-go-for-one-year-to-all-users-in-india)).
- **Midjourney:** bills in dollars ($10–120/month, no free trial) ([eesel.ai](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/midjourney-pricing)). It is the option for a polished look, not for locking a character in.
- **Seedream:** its consumer home, Dreamina, is a ByteDance product, and Seedance launched with India "conspicuously absent" ([Latestly](https://www.latestly.com/technology/where-you-can-access-bytedances-dreamina-seedance-2-0-ai-video-tool-7371082.html)). That leaves only third-party APIs, which is too fiddly for a beginner's main tool **(inference)**.

**The visible Gemini sparkle is the practical snag.** Nano Banana Pro images made by **Free and AI Pro** users carry a visible sparkle watermark. Google removes it only for Ultra subscribers and for API (AI Studio) images. Invisible SynthID and C2PA metadata stay on everything ([implicator.ai](https://www.implicator.ai/google-charges-premium-users-to-remove-the-ai-watermarks-it-calls-critical)). AI Plus is not mentioned, so assume it carries the mark too **(inference)**. The sparkle would carry into image-to-video, so compose with margin and check whether cropping to 1080×1920 removes it. Two questions were not answered by any source: whether cropping out the mark is permitted, and who owns Gemini-app output for commercial use, because Google's consumer terms were not retrieved. **Read them before monetising.**

ChatGPT images carry invisible C2PA metadata plus an imperceptible pixel watermark that, per blog reports, does not restrict commercial licensing ([ChatCut](https://chatcut.io/blog/free-gpt-image-2)). That makes ChatGPT Go the natural backup if the sparkle becomes a problem. For overflow without a visible mark, Nano Banana 2 via the API costs about **$0.067 per 1K image** ([Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/consistent-ai-character-generator-test-2026/)), and FLUX Kontext Pro about $0.04 ([BFL docs](https://docs.eu.bfl.ai/quick_start/pricing); [invideo](https://invideo.io/blog/flux-ai-image-generator/)).

**Identity holds through a fixed reference pack and edits, not re-rolls.** Guides converge on a turnaround sheet (front, side, 3/4 and back views) plus an expression row on a plain background ([selfielabstudio](https://selfielabstudio.com/blog/nano-banana-pro-consistent-character-sheets-guide-20260220)). One vendor guide recommends feeding Nano Banana Pro 4–8 varied references per shot ([selfielabstudio](https://selfielabstudio.com/blog/nano-banana-pro-multi-ref-character-workflow-guide-20260313)). Fix drift with multi-turn edits ("keep everything, change only the expression") rather than fresh generations. Version the pack and never regenerate it **(inference)**.

No source shows any 2026 model rendering **Tamil script** reliably. Vendor claims of about 99% text accuracy list Hindi and Bengali but not Tamil ([sunra.ai](https://sunra.ai/gpt-image-2/text-rendering); [Ideogram](https://ideogram.ai/features/text-rendering/)). So every image prompt says "no text, no letters", and all Tamil goes on in the editor.

**The late-2026 field has narrowed: Sora is gone, Seedance is out of reach, and Kling and Google lead for stylised characters.**

- **Sora:** OpenAI closed the app on 26 April 2026 and removed the API models on 24 September 2026 ([TeamDay](https://www.teamday.ai/blog/best-ai-video-models-2026); [Engadget](https://engadget.com/ai/openai-is-shutting-down-its-sora-video-generation-app-211023358.html)).
- **Kling:** Kling 3.0 launched in February 2026 with 3–15 s clips and start and end frames ([TeamDay](https://www.teamday.ai/blog/best-ai-video-models-2026)). Kling 4.0 followed on 15 October 2026 with no published credit price ([Magic Hour](https://magichour.ai/blog/kling-ai-pricing)).
- **Google:** Gemini Omni Flash was announced at I/O on 19 May 2026. It is included in AI Plus, Pro and Ultra through the Gemini app and Flow, and is "free for YouTube Shorts users" ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/368393/google-unveils-gemini-omni-next-gen-ai-video-builder-simulate-world); [Zapier](https://zapier.com/blog/gemini-omni/)).

Reviewers most often credit Kling for character motion and consistency, Veo for cinematic realism with audio, and Hailuo for cheap iteration ([Pixflow](https://pixflow.net/blog/best-ai-video-generator/)). Most of these comparison pages sell the tools they rank. One comparison says no model guarantees identity and advises checking faces on the **last frame** ([8frame](https://www.8frame.co/blog/best-image-to-video-ai-2026)). **Turn generated audio off, animate few shots, and draw the diagrams.** On the Kling 3.0 API, audio adds 50% ($0.084/s silent vs $0.126/s with audio) ([TeamDay](https://www.teamday.ai/blog/best-ai-video-models-2026)). Sound is on by default for Veo and Kling ([8frame](https://www.8frame.co/blog/best-image-to-video-ai-2026)). Generated speech would be English or gibberish lip-synced over a Tamil voiceover, so mute it **(inference)**.

The biggest cost lever is animating only 3–5 "character moment" shots per Short. Use Ken Burns or parallax moves on stills for the rest. Build labelled anatomy and maps as keyframed graphics in the editor, because AI video is not built for exact labels and graphics re-render for free **(inference)**. Jitter's free tier can't produce the clean master: its paid plan is what adds watermark-free exports ([Jitter Help](https://help.jitter.video/en/articles/5366883-what-is-included-in-jitter-pro)). Do graphics in Resolve instead.

Skip these:

- **Runway:** costs more ($0.12/s) ([TeamDay](https://www.teamday.ai/blog/best-ai-video-models-2026)).
- **All-in-one generators** such as InVideo, Fliki and Revid: they ship template-driven output ([FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/revid-alternatives-2026)), which is close to what YouTube's policy calls "generic or unoriginal templates" ([YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)).

**Google-made clips get a permanent AI tag, which for clay animation should stay in the description.** Since **27 May 2026**, YouTube has auto-labelled significant photorealistic AI content. Labels triggered by C2PA "fully AI-generated" metadata, or by YouTube's own AI tools, cannot be removed. On Shorts, photoreal content gets an **overlay on the video**, while animated or unrealistic AI gets a label only in the **expanded description**. YouTube says labels do not affect recommendations or monetisation ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/27/youtube-will-now-automatically-label-ai-videos/)).

Omni clips carry SynthID, and clips made inside YouTube also carry C2PA ([Zapier](https://zapier.com/blog/gemini-omni/); [MichyDev](https://www.michydev.com/gemini-omni-flash-youtube-create/)). Whether animated content that carries C2PA lands in the description tier is not documented, so test it with one unlisted upload. Omni's availability to Indian creators rests on one low-authority report ([cumbernauld-media](https://cumbernauld-media.com/google-gemini-omni-ai-video-tool-india-launch/)). So does whether it accepts your own still as a start frame. That makes it the first bake-off test, not a plan.

## Free editors finish the job, if Tamil survives the export

**CapCut is out on every surface.** It was not on the 29 June 2020 list of 59 apps. It was blocked in late July 2020, in a second round of about 47 "clone" apps ([Inc42, 5 Aug 2020](https://inc42.com/?p=220316); [OfficeChai](https://officechai.com/stories/xiaomis-browser-app-bytedance-app-capcut-apps-banned-india-initial-ban-59-apps)). A page dated 3 November 2026 reports that CapCut is still absent from official Indian app stores and that VPN users see "Feature not available in your region". The desktop app depends on the same blocked backend, and no reversal has been announced ([jitendra.co](https://jitendra.co/capcut-in-india/)). Adobe Premiere Rush was retired in September 2025 ([Adobe HelpX](https://helpx.adobe.com/ie/premiere-rush/premiere-rush-discontinuation.html)).

**On Windows, DaVinci Resolve (free) is the master editor and Clipchamp the backup.**

- **Resolve free:** watermark-free, with keyframes and a 1080×1920 timeline. Studio costs $295 ([jitendra.co](https://jitendra.co/capcut-in-india/)). Auto "Create Subtitles from Audio" is **Studio-only**, so the free version imports an .srt ([SkyScribe](https://www.sky-scribe.com/en/blog/davinci-resolve-auto-captions-version)).
- **Resolve's Indic text risk:** a Blackmagic forum thread reported that the **Text+** tool mis-shaped Hindi up to v16 while the basic Text title rendered correctly ([Blackmagic Forum](https://forum.blackmagicdesign.com/viewtopic.php?f=32&t=99922)). No Resolve 20 report on Tamil exists either way.
- **Clipchamp:** exports 1080p with no watermark on the free plan, per a blog citing Microsoft ([jitendra.co](https://jitendra.co/capcut-in-india/)) **(unverified)**. Its autocaptions export as .SRT and accept custom fonts. Tamil appears in its interface languages, but not confirmed among caption languages ([Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/clipchamp/how-to-use-autocaptions-in-clipchamp); [Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/clipchamp/how-to-change-the-language-in-clipchamp)).

**On the phone, three free apps are options:**

- **YouTube Create:** officially supports India and needs 4 GB RAM ([YouTube Help 13952912](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13952912?hl=en)).
- **Adobe Premiere for Android:** launched around 22 September 2026, free and watermark-free, needing Android 13+ and 5 GB RAM ([Droid-Life](https://www.droid-life.com/2026/09/22/adobe-premieme-lands-on-android/)). India availability is unconfirmed.
- **Instagram Edits:** its bilingual captions cover 15 languages without Tamil ([Cashify](https://www.cashify.in/news/instagram-edits-new-updates-bilingual-captions-and-more)). Its new Indic caption fonts covered Devanagari and Bengali only ([India TV News](https://www.indiatvnews.com/technology/news/meta-enhances-instagram-reels-and-edits-with-new-features-for-indian-creators-2025-11-30-1019645)).

**Tamil breaks in editors, not in fonts, and it often breaks only on export.** Three apps show the pattern:

- **MAGIX:** a title tool reversed Tamil characters that rendered correctly in Word ([MAGIX forum](https://www.magix.info/us/forum/how-do-i-type-tamil-unicode-or-utf-8-fonts-in-movie-edit-pro-mx-plus--1017430)).
- **Adobe Express:** Tamil looked right in preview and collapsed in the exported file ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/adobe-express-bugs/tamil-font-issue-with-pdf-download-in-adobe-express/idi-p/15267970)).
- **Anki:** switching to Noto Sans Tamil did not fix a ப/வ bug, because the app's shaper was at fault ([Anki forum](https://forums.ankiweb.net/t/tamil-text-in-editor-starting-with-and-not-displayed-correctly-linux-ubuntu/67975)).

Tamil's main risk is vowel signs that sit left of the consonant (கெ, கே, கை) and the க்ஷ and ஸ்ரீ ligatures ([W3C iip #20](https://github.com/w3c/iip/issues/20)). So every editor gets a typed test string, `கை கெ கே கொ கோ கௌ க்ஷ ஸ்ரீ ட்ரீ பொ வெ`, checked in an **exported frame**, not the preview. If an editor fails, pre-render the captions and the verdict stamp as transparent PNGs from a browser, which uses the operating system's shaper, and place them on the timeline **(inference)**.

**Captions: the script is the text, YouTube supplies the timing.** YouTube's automatic captions officially support **Tamil, including on Shorts**, and can be edited in Studio ([YouTube Help 6373554](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6373554)). Off-the-shelf speech-to-text is weak on Tamil. On the FLEURS-Tamil test set, Whisper large-v3 scored **48.58% word error rate**, against 25.49% for AI4Bharat's IndicConformer (self-reported by a model author) ([Hugging Face model card](https://huggingface.co/osmapi/tamil-asr-qwen3)).

Vasanth writes every word, so STT is only for timing **(inference)**. The zero-cost workflow:

1. Upload the rough cut as unlisted.
2. Let YouTube generate Tamil captions.
3. Replace the caption text with the script.
4. Download the .srt. The Studio download step was not confirmed on the help page fetched.
5. Import it into a Resolve subtitle track and burn it in.

The offline backup is Subtitle Edit, which has faster-whisper built in. Users report that pasting the script as the initial prompt "helps — a lot" ([GoTranscript](https://gotranscript.com/public/subtitle-edits-new-faster-whisper-feature-speedy-and-accurate-transcriptions)). Sarvam's Saaras v3 is reported at ₹30 per audio-hour ([smallest.ai](https://smallest.ai/hi/blog/sarvam-ai-speech-to-text-pricing)) **(unverified)**, but its accuracy gain matters little when the script already exists.

**Fonts: Google Fonts' Tamil families are free for monetised video.** Most Google Fonts use the SIL Open Font License 1.1 ([google/fonts](https://github.com/google/fonts)). The heavy-weight caption candidates are Mukta Malar, Catamaran (9 weights), Baloo Thambi 2 and Noto Sans Tamil ([fonttamil.com](https://fonttamil.com/blog/10-best-free-tamil-google-fonts.html)). Install the static .ttf files, because some editors handle variable fonts badly. Legacy non-Unicode Tamil fonts such as Baamini produce garbage in modern tools ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/indesign-discussions/problem-with-tamil-fonts/m-p/13143988)).

**Music and SFX: clear every sound for every platform the master goes to.**

- **YouTube Audio Library:** tracks "won't be claimed" and can be monetised on YouTube. The "Attribution not required" filter avoids credit lines. The page says nothing about use outside YouTube ([YouTube Help 3376882](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3376882?hl=en)).
- **Freesound:** CC0 and CC BY sounds are usable commercially; CC BY-NC is not ([Freesound FAQ](https://freesound.org/help/faq/)).
- **Pixabay:** some composers register tracks with Content ID, so claims happen ([Thematic](https://hellothematic.com/thematic-vs-pixabay/)).
- **Meta Sound Collection:** for Meta products only ([Storrito](https://storrito.com/resources/facebook-sound-collection/)).
- **Epidemic Sound:** costs $9.99/month on annual billing or $17.99 month-to-month ([Photutorial](https://photutorial.com/epidemic-sound-pricing)), and is not needed.

The whoosh and stamp sting go onto one master uploaded to three platforms, so make them **CC0 or self-made** **(inference)**.

## Time per Short sets the cadence, not ambition

**A realistic Short takes 3.3–4.5 hours at steady state and 5–6.5 hours while learning.** No audited case study of an AI-animated Shorts channel exists, so the stage times below are estimates built from the workflow research **(inference)**. They match its 3.3–4.7-hour figure. They are slower than the earlier 2.5–3.5-hour assumption, because consistent-character stills and retakes sit at the slow end of every published workflow ([aiworkflows.tools](https://aiworkflows.tools/blog/complete-guide-ai-short-film-production-2026)). One self-reported creator saw time fall once templates were built ([Medium](https://medium.com/activated-thinker/how-i-built-a-faceless-youtube-channel-that-makes-3-000-month-using-ai-19236c9e105c)).

For scale, Zack D. Films pays freelance 3D animators **$1,000 and 8 days per 20–45 s clip** ([Quickdraw Animation](https://quickdrawanimation.ca/opportunities/job-postings/zack-d-films-animator-wanted)). AI is what makes a solo version possible at all.

| Stage | First ~10 Shorts | Steady state |
|---|---|---|
| Research, script, shot list, prompts | 75–90 min | 45–60 min |
| VO record + Enhance | 30–40 min | 20–30 min |
| Stills (8–10 kept, retakes, QA) | 75–90 min | 45–60 min |
| Image-to-video (4 shots × 2 takes; runs while editing) | 45–60 min | 30–45 min |
| Edit + captions (incl. .srt round trip) | 75–90 min | 45–60 min |
| Publish to YouTube, Instagram, Facebook | 20 min | 10–15 min |
| **Total** | **≈ 5.3–6.5 h** | **≈ 3.3–4.5 h** |
| + Paati line, when used | +20 min | +10 min |
| + each dub language | +20–30 min | +15–20 min |

**Start at 2 Shorts a week and raise cadence only through a time gate.** At 12–15 free hours, two learning-phase Shorts take 10.6–13 hours, which fits. Four at steady state need ≤3.5 hours each. YouTube staff have said there is no reward for posting a set number per day ([OutlierKit transcript](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)). Cadence buys learning speed, not algorithmic favour.

**The gate** **(inference)**:

- **Weeks 1–4:** 2 a week.
- **Step to 3 a week** when the median total time of the last four logged Shorts is **≤4 h**.
- **Step to 4 a week** at **≤3.5 h**, with weekly batching (scripts, then VO, then stills, then queued video, then edits).
- **Treat 5 a week as a ceiling, not a target.** It is reachable only at ≤3 h, which is unlikely before month three.

That yields roughly 30–35 Shorts in the first 90 days rather than about 60. So **run three series first, not five**, so each reaches the four-episode minimum for judging within about five weeks **(inference)**.

## Five conflicts from earlier work, resolved

**(a) The July 2026 "restructure" happened as a clarification, and it changes nothing in practice.**

- **For:** Tubefilter (13 July 2026) reports a three-part structure. It covers generic or repetitive content; a new "unsatisfying or off-putting" category for shock-stitched AI clips and deceptive realistic fakes; and AI personas presenting as human experts on health, finance, legal or political topics. Tubefilter calls it clearer wording rather than new rules ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/07/13/youtube-inauthentic-content-monetization-policy-update/)). TechCrunch (20 July 2026) reports the same three categories, effective 16 July 2026 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/)).
- **Against:** the workflow research found a dated policy timeline that lists no July 2026 change ([AIR.io](https://air.io/en/monetization/youtube-monetization-policy-changes-2026-a-complete-dated-timeline)), and one blog claiming a rename that no other source reported ([NewMoneyMatrix](https://newmoneymatrix.org/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-explained/)). The live Help page is still date-stamped 15 July 2025.
- **The live page itself:** read on 2026-11-04, it uses "Generic or Repetitive Content" as its body heading and contains the AI-persona-on-sensitive-topics rule ([YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)).

**Reconciliation:** YouTube reorganised the wording of the existing page in mid-July 2026 without changing its date stamp **(inference)**. In practice, follow the live page. The kit already covers all three named risks: a distinct story in every Short, no shock compilations, and Paati never presented as a doctor.

**(b) "No licensed music" stays, but not because it raises Vasanth's pay.** YouTube's revenue-sharing page splits the revenue associated with a Short as follows ([YouTube Help 12504220](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220)):

- No music: all of it goes to the country's Creator Pool.
- One track: half goes to the pool.
- Two tracks: one third goes to the pool.

But "your allocation from the Creator Pool is not affected by your use of a music track", and creators keep **45% regardless**. Music shrinks only the shared pool, an effect spread across every monetising creator in India. The rule survives for other reasons:

- Shorts over one minute that contain claimed content are blocked from monetisation (same page).
- Content ID disputes cost time.
- The same master goes to Instagram and Facebook, where YouTube-licensed tracks may not be cleared ([usethirdchair](https://usethirdchair.com/blog/can-you-use-youtube-audio-library-music-on-instagram)).

**(c) Cadence: start at 2 a week, not 4–5.** The earlier 4–5-a-week plan assumed 2.5–3.5 hours per Short. The stage-by-stage estimate is 3.3–4.5 hours at steady state and over 5 while learning, so 4–5 a week would need 13–23 hours against a 12–15-hour budget. Use the time gate in the previous section.

**(d) One safe-zone rule: keep text and key action inside x 90–918 px and y 260–1470 px on 1080×1920.** Published measurements disagree, and none is official for organic posts:

| Platform | Measurement | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Reels | Top 220 px / bottom 450 px blocked | [AdCreate](https://adcreate.com/blog/instagram-reel-dimensions-size-guide-2026) |
| Reels | Top 210 px / bottom 310 px blocked | [Upload-Post](https://www.upload-post.com/tools/safe-zone-checker/) |
| Shorts | Bottom 270 px blocked | [Wild and Free Tools](https://wildandfreetools.com/blog/vertical-video-safe-zones-guide-2026/) |

Taking the most conservative edge of each gives these margins **(inference)**:

- **Bottom:** 450 px (≈23%, replacing the old 20%/384 px).
- **Top:** 260 px.
- **Left:** 90 px.
- **Right:** 162 px (the existing 15%, which clears the action rail).
- **Captions:** in the band y 1150–1450.
- **Verdict stamp and opening question:** in the upper-centre, below y 260.

Preview every upload in each app's draft view, because overlays change with app updates.

**(e) The February 2027 YPP change is confirmed.** YouTube's 10 August 2026 blog post sets the new rules from 1 February 2027 ([YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-partner-program-updates-2027-new-opportunities-earn/)):

- **Entry bar:** 1,000 subscribers plus **20M Shorts views in 90 days or 8,000 watch hours**.
- **Ongoing:** **10M Shorts views in the trailing 90 days** for any channel to earn Shorts revenue in a given month.
- **Unchanged:** Shopping and fan-funding thresholds.

NewscastStudio, vidIQ, The Next Web and PPC Land report the same ([NewscastStudio](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2026/08/10/youtube-partner-program-monetization-changes-2027/); [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-partner-program-changes-2027/); [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-partner-program-doubles-entry-requirements-2027); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-cuts-shorts-pay-for-channels-under-10m-views-from-february-2027/)). For tools, it means Shorts ad money won't pay the subscriptions in year one. Spend should buy learning speed and brand-deal-grade quality, which argues for LEAN until a series proves itself **(inference)**.

## Conclusion

The research turns the toolstack from a shopping problem into a verification problem. Prices are low and mostly rupee-billable. What remains unknown is whether specific tools handle three things that matter to this channel: Tamil speech in a voice changer, Tamil glyphs in an editor's export, and a clay character's face on the last frame of a generated clip. No published source has tested any of them. One weekend of Vasanth's own tests is worth more than any comparison article, and it costs about ₹110.

The bigger shift is that the scarce input is hours, not rupees. A ₹6,100 STANDARD stack cannot make a 4-hour Short take 2 hours. Templates, batching and animating fewer shots can. With the February 2027 bar pushing Shorts ad income well into the future, the best early spend is whatever shortens the loop from idea to measured result. Usually that means LEAN tools and a slower, sustainable cadence that keeps every Short distinct enough to stay on the right side of YouTube's inauthentic-content rules.
