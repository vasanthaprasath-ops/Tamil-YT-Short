Goal set by Vasanth: great content, great quality, built on how viewers think. This note looks for what is still missing after the ep 01–03 work, checks it against our own numbers and fresh research, and proposes fixes. Fixes 1, 3, 4, 6 and 7 were applied to the kit on 2026-11-18 (see `STATUS.md`); 2, 5 and 8 are open for Vasanth.

Evidence grades as elsewhere in the repo: [OFFICIAL] platform's own page, [PEER-REVIEWED], [VENDOR] commissioned or marketing study, [OURS] our own measurement, [UNVERIFIED].

What stands out:

3. **Nobody follows yet.** About 9,500 Instagram and Facebook views produced 0 follows.

## 2. Gaps

### Gap 1 — The rules on the opening contradict each other, and the red-flag rule fired but was not applied
- `kit/checklists.md` §B says: "Sentence 1 states the surprise as a fact (not a question), landing by second 3."
- `kit/checklists.md` §0 (added for ep 03) says: the hook is the searchable question, spoken first, then the surprise "within ~4.5 s".
- Ep 01 and ep 03 both landed the spoken surprise at about 4.2–4.6 s and marked it "NEAR, accepted".
- §E's decision rule ("Viewed % under 60 % → rewrite the first frame and line") was triggered by both published Shorts, but ep 03 kept the same opening shape.

Research:
- Mobile feed attention is measured in fractions of a second: people recall feed content after 0.25 s of exposure, and spent about 1.7 s per feed item on phones (Facebook IQ with Fors Marsh, 2016). [VENDOR, platform-run, old] — [Facebook IQ via Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/facebook-why-mobile-video-ads-must-work-fast/446217/)
- YouTube's own creative guidance (ABCD, built with Ipsos, checked by Kantar on ~11,000 ads) starts with "Attract": quick pacing and 2+ shots in the first 5 s, linked to longer view times. [VENDOR, platform-run, ads not Shorts] — [Google ABCD guide](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/youtube_google_abcd_reference_guide_en.pdf)
- "Most viewers decide in 3 seconds" figures (65 %, 70 %, 71 %) trace back to marketing blogs quoting each other. [UNVERIFIED] — e.g. [TTS Vibes](https://insights.ttsvibes.com/tiktok-first-3-seconds-hook-retention-rate/)
- Curiosity is highest when people are moderately sure of an answer (Kang et al. 2009) — already in `research_notes/Short video formats that work/psychology_attention_sharing.md`. A question everyone *thinks* they know ("why do onions make you cry?") risks the disinterest failure unless the contradiction comes immediately.

A spoken Tamil question takes about 2.4 s at our pace; the surprise then lands after the window in which most people have already decided.

### Gap 2 — No working way to test a hook
- §D says "try Trial Reels for hook tests". Instagram does not publish a threshold, but the detailed guides say Trial Reels need a professional account with about 200 followers (some say 1,000). [UNVERIFIED] — [Planoly](https://www.planoly.com/blog/how-to-post-a-trial-reel-on-instagram), [Superprofile](https://superprofile.bio/guides/instagram-trial-reels). We have 0, so the test route probably does not exist for us yet.
- YouTube Shorts have no A/B test; re-uploading to test is banned by our own rule.
- So the only test left is episode to episode, which only works if one thing changes at a time. Ep 03 changed the voice model, character design, camera, pace and hook wording together.

### Gap 3 — The health series and YouTube's AI-persona rule
YouTube's monetization policy page (updated 15 July 2025, clarified July 2026) says: "This policy refers to channels that use AI-generated personas to deliver information on sensitive topics… content that presents itself as a human expert providing advice to viewers on topics such as health", example "An AI 'doctor' providing medical diagnoses, health advice, or wellness remedies"; such channels "will not be allowed to monetize". It allows "using AI to visualize a unique character". [OFFICIAL] — [YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392); July 2026 coverage: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/)

Our narrator is not presented as a doctor and Mani is a cartoon, so we are probably outside the rule today. But the plan puts about 75 % of the next Shorts in the body/health series, all narrated by an AI voice, and the policy does not say where the line is or what "too much" means. `PROJECT-CONTEXT.md` already keeps Paati from appearing as a doctor; the narrator and the health tips need the same guard written down.

### Gap 4 — AI voice: trust and disclosure
- Stated trust favours human voices: human voices scored 23 % higher on trust in a matched test of 1,300 people, and trust dropped 27 % when listeners were told to expect an AI voice. [VENDOR, Studio Resonate] — [ContentGrip](https://www.contentgrip.com/studio-resonate-ai-voice-study/)
- Behaviour is closer to parity: synthetic voice-over ads matched human ones on engagement and purchase intent (WPP/Choreograph); blind radio listeners rated AI and human about equal. [VENDOR] — [WPP Media](https://www.wppmedia.com/news/ai-voices-audio-ads), [Radio Ink](https://radioink.com/2026/07/07/radio-listeners-cant-detect-ai-voice-but-dont-trust-it-either/)
- We switch the AI label on for Instagram and Facebook, which is the "primed to expect AI" condition.
- No study in Tamil or any Indian language (unchanged from the craft playbook).

Reading: an AI voice probably costs little in watching behaviour, but it may cost trust on exactly the topics where trust matters most (health), and it is the one thing that could pull the health series toward Gap 3. Open decision 2 in `STATUS.md` (own voice vs ElevenLabs) is now a real decision, not a preference.

### Gap 5 — Measurement is in free text, at different ages

### Gap 7 — Topic choice uses one platform's view
The "75 % body for the next 8 Shorts" plan was set before any data. The first data says body topics travel on YouTube and local-identity topics get shared on Instagram. With one Short each, neither is proven, which is the reason not to commit 75 % yet.

## 3. Fixes (proposed, in priority order)

1. **The 2-second opening rule** (fixes Gap 1). Replace the two conflicting lines with one:
   - The surprise is **spoken by 2.0 s** (about 14 syllables at our pace) and **visible from frame 0**.
   - The searchable question stays **on screen** from frame 0 and in the title; it is spoken only if it fits in ~1.5 s, otherwise the spoken line opens with the surprise.
   - Frame 0 shows the contradiction itself (the thing the surprise is about), not a mood shot.
   - Ep 04 changes **only this**; everything else copies ep 03. Compare Viewed % at 48 h and 7 days with ep 01–03.
2. **A 5-person first-second test** before the voice is made (fixes Gap 2 until Trial Reels unlock). Show frame 0 and the first 2 s (with the planned line read aloud) to 3–5 people who have not seen the script, inside a phone feed of other Shorts. Ask: "What is this about?" and "Would you keep watching?" Two or more "swipe" answers → rewrite the opening. [PRACTITIONER method, no data; cheap]
3. **One change per episode**, named in `kit/improvement-log.md` before the build, so the next number means something.
4. **Health guard** (fixes Gap 3), added to checklist §B and the producer skill:
   - The narrator never claims to be a doctor or expert, and no character is shown as one.
   - Explain *why*, never prescribe: no remedies, doses or "do this to cure"; at most a "see a doctor if…" line.
   - Sources on screen or in the description for every health claim.
   - Alternate health and non-health topics, so the channel never becomes "an AI voice giving health information".
5. **Decide the voice for the health series** (Gap 4): either Vasanth's own voice for உடம்பு episodes, or keep Prashant V with fix 4 strictly applied. A human voice removes the AI-persona question and the trust gap; the cost is recording time. Vasanth's call.
6. **Structured metrics at fixed ages** (Gap 5): add `read_age_h` and `ig_skip_rate_pct` columns; fill the existing columns instead of the notes; read every Short at 48 h and 7 days; log minutes per stage from ep 04.
7. **Make following worth it** (Gap 6):
   - Confirm the "related video" and series playlist are set on ep 01–02 (desktop YouTube Studio).
   - Pin the best-shared Reel (ep 02) to the top of the Instagram grid.
   - The pinned comment and caption name what the series does next ("அடுத்த உடம்பு கேள்வி: …").
8. **Test the topic split for 4 Shorts** (Gap 7): alternate body and city (2 + 2) before fixing the 75 % mix, and judge each series on its own platform's numbers (YouTube views and Viewed %, Instagram share rate).

## 4. What this note does not cover
- No peer-reviewed study was found on the first seconds of short video, on Shorts subscriber conversion, or on AI voices in Tamil. The opening rule rests on our own numbers plus platform-run ad research; treat it as a test, not a law.
- The AI-persona rule's boundary is not published; re-read the policy page before scaling the health series.
