# Hooks and scripts for faceless narrated Shorts: an evidence-graded playbook (researched 2026-11-13)

Grading key: **[OFFICIAL]** platform source · **[PEER-REVIEWED]** · **[PRACTITIONER-DATA]** named creator with numbers · **[PRACTITIONER-OPINION]** · **[VENDOR]** tool or agency blog/dataset · **[MYTH/UNVERIFIED]**

Scope note: this goes deeper than `research_notes/Shorts scriptwriting craft.md` and validates it. The psychology of curiosity and sharing (Loewenstein, Berger & Milkman) and the Debunking Handbook are covered elsewhere and are not repeated here.

**Main finding.** Very little hard evidence exists for *specific* hook wordings. The strongest evidence is about three things:

1. The swipe decision happens at the very start. YouTube reports it as a metric, Galloway has thresholds for it, and one peer-reviewed study shows early drop-off.
2. Holding viewers to the end matters. Galloway found a link between length/AVD and views, and Hoyos has numbers from her own channel.
3. Clarity and simplicity. Most "hook formula" lists are folklore. The largest vendor dataset (121k hooks) found only a ~31% spread between the best and worst hook *type*.

---

## 1. Hooks in the first 1–3 s (visual vs verbal vs text, alignment, hook types, foreshadowing)

### Takeaway
That the opening seconds decide the swipe is well supported. YouTube measures it directly as "viewed vs swiped away", and Galloway's data puts winning Shorts at 70–90% on that metric. *Which* hook wording wins has almost no evidence: hook-type differences in the largest dataset are small, and the "question vs statement" and "you" effects are not established. The practical rules are: (a) a clear single subject the viewer understands with the sound off, (b) visual, spoken and on-screen text all saying the same thing, (c) a spoken foreshadow line straight after the hook. These rest on practitioner opinion backed by some personal numbers.

### Cited findings
- **[OFFICIAL]** YouTube Studio reports "Viewed (vs. swiped away)", the share of times viewers watched a Short rather than swiping past, and a "Shown in feed" count. YouTube publishes no target value. — [YouTube Help: video reach](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314355?hl=en)
- **[OFFICIAL]** YouTube defines a "Stayed to watch" metric: "the percentage of times viewers stayed to watch past the initial seconds of a Short". — [YouTube Help: content performance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281)
- **[OFFICIAL, paraphrased secondhand]** In an interview with Rene Ritchie, YouTube Shorts product lead Todd Sherman said a Short is more likely to be shown to others when a higher share of people who see it in the feed watch rather than swipe. They also said average view duration and watch time weigh heavily. Buffer paraphrases this without direct quotes or a link to the interview. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)
- **[PRACTITIONER-DATA]** Paddy Galloway, 33 channels, 5,400 Shorts, ~3.3B views (Apr 2023): Shorts under 60% viewed-vs-swiped "rarely performed well". Top performers were mostly at 70–90%, but a high rate did not guarantee success. His advice: make the first second punchy and treat the intro "like a thumbnail". — [Galloway thread (Thread Reader)](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html)
- **[PRACTITIONER-DATA]** Jenny Hoyos: the average scroll-through (viewed) rate is about 70%, and hers is about 85%. She calls 90%+ retention "the bare minimum" for a Short to blow up, depending on impressions. — [Creator Science podcast](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)
- **[PRACTITIONER-OPINION]** Hoyos' hook tests:
  - It must work as a long-form title and thumbnail.
  - It must be visual and understandable on mute.
  - Use simple words at a 5th-grade level or below, checked with readabilityformulas.com. Jargon like "business" pushes text toward 8th grade, so define terms.
  - Copy proven hook formats first, then add your own twist.

  — [Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)
- **[PRACTITIONER-OPINION]** Hoyos' structure is hook (very short), then a **2-line spoken foreshadow**, usually ≤3 s, that sets up the ending, then a smooth transition, then the payoff. — [Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)
- **[PRACTITIONER-OPINION]** Kallaway's hook rules:
  - Name one clear subject and plant one question in every viewer's head.
  - The visual, spoken and text hooks must align.
  - "Contrast" means the distance between what the viewer currently believes and a contrarian or alternative view: "the bigger the contrast, the more the curiosity". He supports this only with his own view counts, and no study isolates it.

  — summaries of his hook video via [Recapio](https://recapio.com/digest/how-to-create-a-killer-hook-impossible-to-skip-by-kallaway) and [Wayfind](https://wayfindknow.substack.com/p/irresistible-hook); search summary of [Lilys notes](https://lilys.ai/notes/841043)
- **[VENDOR]** quso.ai sorted 121,820 short-form hooks into 11 types with AI and ranked them by median views:
  - Social proof/fame drop 232, product reveal 211, personal reveal 210, shocking stat 209, future stakes 203, generic 203, open loop 201, curiosity question 197, counter-claim 196, direct command 182, authority 177.
  - The best-to-worst spread is only ~31%. The authors themselves say a weak or slow opening matters more than which type you pick.
  - It does not name platforms, gives no confidence intervals, and quso sells clipping tools.

  — [quso.ai research](https://quso.ai/research/best-hooks-for-short-form)
- **[VENDOR]** OverseerOS looked at 4,784 hooks from YouTube videos with 1M+ views (1,467 Shorts), snapshot Aug 2026:
  - The median hook is 12 words (Shorts 11). Only 15.4% are questions, 32.5% use "you", and 14.2% contain digits.
  - In the 73 channels that make both formats, the question rate is identical: Shorts 10.5% vs long-form 10.7%. The pooled gap was a niche-mix artefact.
  - Among Shorts, the question rate is *lower* in the 10M+ tier (16.2%) than in lower tiers (~32.8%). Use of "you" falls at higher tiers.
  - The study had no control group. It cannot show causation, and it does not measure visuals.

  — [OverseerOS study](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/million-view-youtube-hooks-study)
- **[PEER-REVIEWED]** Salminen, Wahid, Yang & Jansen (ACM Hypertext '24) studied TikTok ad data from one e-commerce advertiser. Most viewers left within the **first quarter** of the video. This supports early drop-off as real, but it is one advertiser's ads, not organic explainers. — [JYX repository](https://jyx.jyu.fi/handle/123456789/98478), DOI 10.1145/3648188.3677048
- **[PRACTITIONER-OPINION]** Brendan Kane's *Hook Point* says viewers decide in about 3 seconds, a "hook point" can be a format, phrase, image or concept, and no hook is known to work until tested. These come from secondary book summaries. — [Summaries.com](https://www.summaries.com/blog/hook-point); [Blinkist](https://www.blinkist.com/en/books/hook-point-en)
- **[OFFICIAL, secondhand]** TikTok's advertiser guidance reportedly says to state the proposition in the first 3 s and prioritise the hook in the first 6 s. I did not reach the primary Creative Center page. — via search summary of [Stackmatix](https://stackmatix.com/blog/tiktok-creative-best-practices-2026)
- **[MYTH/UNVERIFIED]** These widely repeated statistics have no traceable study:
  - "84% of a video's final views are predicted by the share who stay past the third second" — [dev.to](https://dev.to/bulkdl/hook-engineering-the-science-of-the-first-3-seconds-in-short-form-video-2026-2jp9)
  - "strong 3-second hook = 2.4× more likely to hit FYP", "70–85% 3-s hold = 2.2× views", "33% scroll in first 3 s", "half of ad impact in first 2 s"

  These were surfaced in search via [Virlo](https://virlo.ai/blog/why-the-first-few-seconds-matter) and [Teleprompter](https://teleprompter.com/blog/tiktok-3-second-rule).

### Inferences
- For a Zack D. Films–style explainer, the *visual* hook carries most of the weight. Kallaway's alignment and Hoyos' mute test agree with Zack D.'s practice of the needle at the skin in frame 1 (see §4). Spoken Tamil cannot be relied on in the first second for viewers who scroll on mute or don't speak Tamil, so frame 1 plus on-screen text must name the subject alone.
- "Question vs statement" and "you vs I" choices should be treated as A/B variables, not rules. The only large datasets show small or no effects, and they are confounded by niche.
- Foreshadowing straight after the hook is the most transferable "formula". It costs about 2 lines, and it gives viewers the progress mechanism that Hoyos credits for her retention.

### Gaps
- I found no controlled test (A/B on the same video) comparing visual vs verbal vs text hooks, or question vs statement hooks, on Shorts.
- I could not retrieve TikTok Creative Center primary pages or Kallaway's own dataset, if one exists.
- I found nothing specific to Tamil or Indian-language hook performance.

## 2. Retention techniques in the body (open loops, re-hooks, but/therefore, stakes, pacing, curiosity gaps)

### Takeaway
Most body techniques are practitioner opinion with no isolated data: but/therefore, visible mechanisms, re-hooks and "no dull moments". The only measured link is outcome-level. Galloway found longer AVD went with more views, and Hoyos showed cutting seconds raised retention. Use these techniques as craft defaults. Do not believe any specific numbers attached to them, such as "re-hook every 7 seconds".

### Cited findings
- **[PRACTITIONER-OPINION]** Hoyos uses "but/therefore" storytelling, where each beat causes the next, or a problem→solution shape. She uses a **three-step list as a visible mechanism** so viewers know how close the payoff is, and sets an expectation followed by a twist. — [Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)
- **[PRACTITIONER-OPINION]** Hoyos also uses a "pacing break without the pacing actually breaking" as the transition after the foreshadow. — [Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)
- **[PRACTITIONER-OPINION]** The "but/therefore" rule originates with Trey Parker and Matt Stone. It is a writing heuristic and was never tested on Shorts. — [Perell](https://perell.com/note/but-therefore-rule/) (cited in the existing notes)
- **[MYTH/UNVERIFIED]** The "MrBeast production guide" reportedly stresses "no dull moments", a "wow factor", CTR/AVD/AVP as core metrics, and smooth transitions after the hook. It also says: "We lost 21 million viewers in the first minute." Its authenticity is **unconfirmed**, it is about long-form, and it says nothing about Shorts. — [Creator Handbook](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/leaked-document-allegedly-reveals-mrbeasts-secrets-to-youtube-success-the-key-takeaways/)
- **[PRACTITIONER-DATA]** Galloway found "no strong relationships" between likes, shares or comments and performance in his 2023 sample. Watch duration was what correlated with views. — [Galloway thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html)
- **[PRACTITIONER-DATA, n=1 anecdote]** Hoyos had one Short with a share-to-view ratio of ~20% and 92% growth. Another got ~10M views despite 70% retention on its first 100k views. She calls both hunches. — [Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)
- **[PRACTITIONER-OPINION]** Kane says to build tension by creating a problem the viewer wants resolved. He also warns that over-optimising for 3-second engagement without delivering trains audiences to disengage. — [Summaries.com](https://www.summaries.com/blog/hook-point)

### Inferences
- For explainers, "but/therefore" maps neatly onto a myth-busting arc: the belief, *but* the evidence, *therefore* the real answer. The Paati verdict format already has this shape.
- Specific re-hook timings ("every 5/7/10 s") appear only in vendor blogs and should be ignored. Prefer the rule *every line changes the situation*, and check it in the retention graph.
- Galloway's finding that likes and comments don't correlate, together with Hoyos' share hunch, suggests that shares matter more than comments for distribution. This is unproven. It is consistent with the Berger & Milkman notes already in the project, which this does not contradict.

### Gaps
- I found no data isolating open loops, re-hooks or mid-video stakes on Shorts retention.
- I could not find a primary YouTube statement on whether shares are a Shorts ranking signal.

## 3. Script economy (word count, spoken vs written, readability, sentence length, ideal length)

### Takeaway
The one quantified readability rule is Hoyos' 5th-grade target, which is her personal practice. On length, Galloway's data shows longer, well-held Shorts averaged more views, but it is correlational. YouTube's stated position, relayed secondhand, is that "there's no ideal length; the story sets it". Hoyos' sweet spot is ~34 s on YouTube, and she found *shorter* (10–20 s) worked better on TikTok. Length is platform-specific and earned by retention.

### Cited findings
- **[PRACTITIONER-DATA]** Galloway:
  - Most Shorts in his sample were 20–40 s, and very short ones were rare.
  - Longer Shorts did much better on average, a "clear relationship".
  - Shorts with **AVD above 50 s averaged 4.1M views**.
  - Outliers existed at every length, and he checked medians as well as means.
  - He calls it "early research… subject to more testing".

  — [Galloway thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html)
- **Correction to existing notes:** secondary write-ups say "50–60 s Shorts averaged 1.7M views" ([Digital Information World](https://www.digitalinformationworld.com/2023/04/paddy-galloway-decoded-youtube-shorts.html)). Other sites credit a 5,400-Short / 50–60 s finding to an "Inflow Network" study ([search results incl. Piktochart](https://piktochart.com/blog/how-long-youtube-shorts/)). The primary thread, as fetched, gives the AVD>50 s = 4.1M figure. Cite Galloway's thread directly and treat the 1.7M and Inflow attributions as **[UNVERIFIED]**. Bytecap, the source for the length claim in the existing notes, returned 403 and could not be verified.
- **[PRACTITIONER-DATA]** Hoyos:
  - Her most popular Shorts are ~34 s, and she aims for that on YouTube.
  - She claims Shorts under 30 s "need over 100% retention" to take off for her audience.
  - On TikTok, videos over 30 s underperformed, and 10–20 s of dense information worked better.
  - On a 32-s Short, 1 s is ~3% of runtime, so "every single second" matters.

  — [Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)
- **[OFFICIAL, paraphrased]** YouTube (Sherman/Ritchie via Buffer) says there is no ideal length and the story should set it. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)
- **[VENDOR]** Socialinsider's 2024 TikTok completion rates by length: 92% (<15 s), 84% (16–30 s), 68% (31–60 s). These are secondhand, and I could not check the method. — via search summary of [Virlo](https://virlo.ai/blog/why-the-first-few-seconds-matter)
- **[VENDOR]** Median hook length in 1M+ view Shorts is 11 words (descriptive only). — [OverseerOS](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/million-view-youtube-hooks-study)

### Inferences
- Completion rate falls as length rises, while total watch time and views rise with length if retention holds. That is the trade-off behind the "length only pays when every second earns it" rule in the existing notes, and the existing rule stands. For a new Tamil channel without proven retention, ~30–40 s is the defensible default. Go longer only when the retention graph stays flat.
- Readability formulas such as Flesch and readabilityformulas.com are English-only. For Tamil, the transferable rule is spoken colloquial Tamil, short clauses, and everyday words instead of Sanskritised or written (செந்தமிழ்) vocabulary. Explain any technical term in one breath. **[Inference, no Tamil-specific source found.]**
- Words-per-second: no sourced figure was found. Measure the TTS/narrator pace for the channel and derive word budgets from it, rather than importing English WPM numbers. Tamil words are longer and more agglutinative, so word counts don't transfer.

### Gaps
- I found no primary source for spoken words-per-minute targets in Shorts.
- I found no readability tooling or evidence for Tamil.
- Galloway's full white paper, promised in the thread, was not located.

## 4. Payoffs and endings (end right after the promise, twist, loop endings)

### Takeaway
"End right after the payoff" is the best-supported ending rule. It has Hoyos' numeric anecdote (83%→88% retention from cutting one second) and matches Zack D. Films' practice. Loop endings are **not evidenced**:
- YouTube has made no statement that loops boost distribution.
- Since 31 Mar 2025, replays count toward *public* views but not toward "engaged views", which drive monetisation.

Loops may inflate the view counter and retention >100%, but there is no proof they raise reach.

### Cited findings
- **[PRACTITIONER-DATA, n=1]** Hoyos: "Whatever you say you're gonna do, you end it right after you do it." Trimming one second from an ending moved retention from 83% to 88%, and the video took off. She writes the last line before filming. Rewatches are how retention climbs above the scroll-through rate. — [Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)
- **[PRACTITIONER-OPINION]** Hoyos: set an expectation, then twist it. — [Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/); [Marketing Examined](https://marketing-examined.beehiiv.com/p/jenny-hoyos-shortform-video-playbook)
- **[PRACTITIONER-OPINION]** A breakdown of Zack D. Films' "How Stitches Work" (31 s, ~253M views; 27.8M channel subs per the author):
  - The question is posed in the first second, with the needle already at the skin.
  - The video ends "where curiosity ends".
  - It has **no loop claimed and no CTA**, and the description is empty.
  - All figures are unsourced in the post, and the reading of the structure is the author's.

  — [Rusnok Substack](https://danielrusnok.substack.com/p/how-a-faceless-channel-engineered)
- **[OFFICIAL, via press]** From 31 Mar 2025, a Short's public view counts each time it starts or replays, with no minimum watch time. The old count became "engaged views", which excludes loops, and YPP eligibility and revenue share stay based on engaged views. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted); [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/03/26/youtube-shorts-views-counting-stats/)
- **[OFFICIAL]** YouTube Help defines engaged views as "not including any loops". — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281)
- **[MYTH/UNVERIFIED]** "Loops/replays are one of the highest-weighted Shorts signals" appears in third-party blogs without attribution, and no YouTube staff statement was found. — e.g. [vidIQ blog on looping](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/double-youtube-watch-time-looping-shorts) (vendor), per search summary

### Inferences
- Existing checklist item "last line flows into the first so the video loops" should be **downgraded to optional/untested**. It can't hurt if the ending still lands, but it is not a proven lever. Don't sacrifice a clean payoff for a seamless loop.
- "Retention >100%" in Hoyos' talk comes from rewatches. That is why short videos can show >100% average viewed. It does not by itself show causation for reach.

### Gaps
- I found no controlled data on loop vs non-loop endings, and no YouTube statement on whether rewatches feed ranking.

## 5. CTAs ("follow/subscribe" asks in Shorts)

### Takeaway
No controlled evidence exists either way. The only data point is Galloway's: long-form converts more subscribers per 10k views on average, but 1M+ subscriber channels convert better *on Shorts*. He speculates this may be due to better Shorts and stronger CTAs, which is his guess, not a finding. Creator anecdotes say spoken CTAs show up as dips in the retention graph. Zack D. Films, the model channel here, uses none. The default: no spoken subscribe ask. If you use one, keep the visuals and payoff running underneath it, and test it.

### Cited findings
- **[PRACTITIONER-DATA]** Galloway:
  - Long-form converts more subs per 10k views than Shorts on average. A secondary summary gives 22 vs 17 ([Digital Information World](https://www.digitalinformationworld.com/2023/04/paddy-galloway-decoded-youtube-shorts.html)).
  - For channels with 1M+ subs, Shorts convert better. He *theorises* this is from better Shorts and stronger CTAs.

  — [Galloway thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html)
- **[PRACTITIONER-OPINION]** CTAs tend to coincide with dips in the retention graph. Keep the story's end result playing during any ask. The estimated gain from fixing this is a modest 5–10%. — [Shorts-faceless newsletter, via search summary](https://shorts-faceless.beehiiv.com/p/getting-viral-shorts-isnt-fcking-hard)
- **[VENDOR]** A dip at a CTA may just mean the video had already delivered its value, so causation is unclear. — [ContentStudio](https://contentstudio.io/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics-guide) (per search summary)
- **[VENDOR, conflicting]** Some guides recommend a spoken CTA in the first 15 s, and others a subtle content-related question at the end. Neither has data. — [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-shorts-algorithm-explained) (per search summary)
- **[PRACTITIONER-OPINION]** Zack D. Films: no CTA, empty description. — [Rusnok](https://danielrusnok.substack.com/p/how-a-faceless-channel-engineered)

### Inferences
- In the existing notes, rule 8 (end at payoff) implicitly argues against a tacked-on spoken CTA. That is consistent with this evidence.
- A low-cost alternative is an on-screen or channel-identity cue (recurring character, verdict stamp, series name) that earns follows without a separate retention-costing beat. This is an inference only.

### Gaps
- I found no A/B or platform data on CTA vs no CTA in Shorts subscriber conversion.

## 6. Common script mistakes that kill retention

### Takeaway
The recurring, multi-source failure modes are:
- a vague or multi-subject hook
- visual, spoken and text hooks that don't match
- a slow or abstract opening
- jargon (high reading level)
- "and then" filler beats
- running past the payoff (endings, CTAs, recap)
- hook bait the body doesn't deliver

All are practitioner-level evidence. Only "slow opening" and "running past the payoff" have any numbers behind them.

### Cited findings
- **[PRACTITIONER-OPINION]** Vague hook means each viewer imagines a different subject and leaves. Misaligned hooks have the same effect. — Kallaway via [Recapio](https://recapio.com/digest/how-to-create-a-killer-hook-impossible-to-skip-by-kallaway)
- **[PRACTITIONER-DATA]** Viewed-vs-swiped below 60% rarely performs, which points to a slow or weak first second. — [Galloway](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html)
- **[VENDOR]** A weak or slow opening matters more than which hook archetype you pick (~31% spread across types). — [quso.ai](https://quso.ai/research/best-hooks-for-short-form)
- **[PRACTITIONER-DATA, n=1]** Lingering 1 s past the payoff cost 5 points of retention (83% vs 88%). — [Hoyos, Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)
- **[PRACTITIONER-OPINION]** Jargon pushes the reading level up and loses viewers, so define terms instead. — [Hoyos](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)
- **[PRACTITIONER-OPINION]** Clickbait hooks that don't deliver train audiences to disengage. — Kane via [Summaries.com](https://www.summaries.com/blog/hook-point)
- **[OFFICIAL, adjacent]** In Oct 2026 YouTube reduced Shorts reach for channels that re-upload others' videos and urged "fresh storytelling" and "unique analysis". This is relevant to faceless channels that lean on stock or reused clips. — [Android Headlines](https://www.androidheadlines.com/2026/10/youtube-cuts-reach-reuploaded-shorts-originality-push.html)

### Inferences
- **Validation of existing notes:** rules 1–9 and 12 are consistent with the sources, all at practitioner level.
  - Rule 10 (length) needs the Galloway figure corrected: cite AVD>50 s → 4.1M avg, not "50–60 s averaged more", and drop the unverifiable Bytecap citation.
  - Rule 11 (MrBeast guide) should stay flagged as unverified and long-form-only.
  - The loop item in the checklist should become "optional, untested".
- **For a Tamil faceless channel:** hooks must survive mute, because frame 1 plus text names the subject. Use spoken colloquial Tamil, not written Tamil. On-screen text should be short Tamil or Tanglish keywords that match the narration word for word. Keep original animation, not reused clips, given YouTube's 2026 originality push.

### Gaps
- I found no study of Shorts retention specific to Indian or Tamil audiences.
- I found no primary Hormozi short-form hook guidance. Results were only third-party "Hormozi-inspired" frameworks such as [Opus](https://www.opus.pro/blog/3-easy-ways-to-make-viral-youtube-shorts-like-alex-hormozi), so nothing here is attributed to him.
- No vidIQ or Creator Insider primary video with hook data was retrieved.
