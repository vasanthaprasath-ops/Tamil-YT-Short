# Production and packaging craft for faceless animated explainer Shorts/Reels (2023–2026)

Grading legend: [OFFICIAL] platform's own help or newsroom; [PEER-REVIEWED]; [PRACTITIONER-DATA] a named creator or analyst with numbers; [PRACTITIONER-OPINION]; [VENDOR] a tool seller or platform-commissioned marketing study; [MYTH/UNVERIFIED].

Research limits: 16 tool calls. Few official pages were fetched directly; most platform claims below arrive through secondary pages and are graded on that basis. **"Confirms kit"** and **"contradicts kit"** refer to `kit/style-sheet.md` and `reports/Tamil Shorts production toolstack.md`.

## Voice-over: pace, energy, tone, AI vs human, loudness

### Takeaway
No source gives a retention-optimal words-per-minute figure for Shorts. Comprehension research says normal narration (~150 wpm) can go up to about 2x before memory drops, so speed is not the risk; complexity and older listeners are. AI vs human voice studies are mixed and mostly vendor-funded: listeners often can't tell the difference, but they say they trust humans more. That supports the kit's "own voice" rule. −14 LUFS is folklore-grade: widely repeated, never found on a YouTube page.

### Cited Findings
- Typical 1x narration is about 150 wpm. Comprehension tends to decline around 200–270 wpm (one older estimate says 275). [PEER-REVIEWED, via press summary] — [ScienceDaily (Murphy et al., UCLA, Applied Cognitive Psychology 2022)](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2022/01/220111153637.htm)
- In the UCLA study, lectures at 1x scored 26/40 against 25/40 at 2x on the immediate test. A week later scores were 24 (1x), 21 (1.5x and 2x) and 20 (2.5x). Little effect below 2x, but the authors caution that harder material may differ. [PEER-REVIEWED] — [ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2022/01/220111153637.htm); [phys.org PDF](https://phys.org/news/2022-01-students-class-videos.pdf)
- Older adults are hurt more by faster playback than younger adults, and older listeners prefer slower speech. [PEER-REVIEWED] — [Castel lab, UCLA (PDF)](https://castel.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/111/2023/04/The-effect-of-video-playback-speed-on-learning-and-mind-wandering-in-younger-and-older-adults.pdf)
- Jenny Hoyos writes scripts at a **5th-grade readability level or lower**, checked on readabilityformulas.com. Top Shorts she scraped, including MrBeast's, scored about 1st grade. One jargon word can move a script from grade 5 to grade 8, so she defines terms instead. [PRACTITIONER-DATA] — [Creator Science ep. 167](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)
- Hoyos replaces filler transitions ("let's get started") with a story beat ("so I cooked illegally"), a "pacing break without the pacing breaking". She ends on the reaction and cuts abruptly. [PRACTITIONER-OPINION] — [Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)
- **AI vs human, workplace training (2025):** viewers focused on audio quality more than on whether the voice was human or AI. [VENDOR/trade press; study design not inspected] — [Training Magazine](https://trainingmag.com/leveraging-ai-avatars-and-voices-for-scalable-trusted-learning/)
- **AI vs human, audiobooks:** an Edison Research study funded by AI-audio firm Spoken found humans rated higher for **exposition** without multiple characters, and AI multi-cast rated higher for character fiction. [VENDOR] — [Podnews press release](https://podnews.net/press-release/edison-spoken)
- **AI vs human, radio (2026):** in blind listening, trust scores were nearly identical, but opinions shifted once listeners learned who or what was speaking. [VENDOR/trade press] — [Radio Ink](https://radioink.com/2026/07/07/radio-listeners-cant-detect-ai-voice-but-dont-trust-it-either/)
- **AI vs human, survey:** 77% of consumers trust human voices most, and 14% distrust any voice they believe is AI. [VENDOR survey, Adobe Express] — [ContentGrip](https://www.contentgrip.com/ai-voices-in-marketing-adobe/)
- **AI vs human, ads:** AI and human ad voices got identical authenticity ratings (75%), and regionally accented AI voices did better on recommendation. [VENDOR, Azerion] — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/azerion-study-finds-ai-ad-voices-fool-37-of-listeners-into-thinking-human/)
- **Platform treatment:** YouTube's monetisation policy targets "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates" and does not ban TTS as such. [OFFICIAL, already in kit] — [YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- **Loudness:** many sources say YouTube normalises to −14 LUFS. One admits YouTube "doesn't publicly mention its loudness standard anywhere". The mobile "Stable Volume" toggle adds further real-time compression. [MYTH/UNVERIFIED as an official figure; PRACTITIONER-OPINION as practice] — [videvo](https://videvo.net/blog/?p=40770); [Production Advice](https://productionadvice.co.uk/youtube-loudness/); [hearably](https://hearably-web.pages.dev/blog/normalize-youtube-audio/)

### Inferences
- **Confirms kit:** own voice, calm conversational tone, −14 LUFS / −1 dBTP labelled "common guidance, not official".
- Tamil speech packs more syllables per word than English, so wpm doesn't transfer. Use readability (short words, no jargon) and a natural cousin-explaining pace instead of a wpm target.
- The 1x–2x comprehension result means the real risk for a fast VO is not speed but dense jargon. Paati-age and older family viewers are the group most hurt by fast delivery.
- "Pause before the payoff" has no data behind it. Hoyos's practice points the other way: strip dead time, end the instant the payoff lands.
- The Stable Volume toggle squashes dynamics, so keep the VO well above any bed (see Sound).

### Gaps
- No study measures wpm against Shorts retention or swipe rate. Any figure such as "160–180 wpm is optimal" would be [MYTH/UNVERIFIED].
- No evidence that YouTube or Instagram treat AI narration differently in distribution, beyond the templated-content policy.
- No official YouTube or Instagram LUFS page was located. Spotify's −14 LUFS page was not fetched.
- No AI-vs-human voice study in Tamil or any Indian language.

## Captions and on-screen text

### Takeaway
The famous "85% watch without sound" stat is a 2016 Facebook figure that Digiday relayed from a few publishers. It is not a platform measurement and says nothing about Shorts or Reels. TikTok's "88%" is an opinion survey ("sound is essential"), not a sound-on share. Captions still help, but the best evidence is a policy review whose universality has been challenged. No research tests word-by-word or keyword-highlighted kinetic captions.

### Cited Findings
- **The "85%" stat** comes from Digiday (May 2016) and was **publisher-reported**: LittleThings 85%, Mic 85% of 30-second views, PopSugar 50–80%, MEC 85–90% for branded video. It is not from Facebook. [MYTH/UNVERIFIED as a platform stat] — [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/silent-world-facebook-video/); [Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2016/05/17/85-percent-facebook-video-views-without-volume/)
- **The TikTok "88%"** is a Kantar study commissioned by TikTok: "88% of TikTok users said that sound is essential to the TikTok experience." It is often misquoted as "88% watch with sound on". [VENDOR] — [TikTok for Business blog](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/kantar-report-how-brands-are-making-noise-and-driving-impact-with-sound-on-tiktok)
- **Gernsbacher (2015), "Video Captions Benefit Everyone"** (Policy Insights from the Behavioral and Brain Sciences): over 100 studies show captions improve comprehension, attention and memory, with the largest gains for non-native-language viewers, early readers and deaf or hard-of-hearing viewers. [PEER-REVIEWED, review] — [PDF](https://successforkidswithhearingloss.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/Video-Captions-Benefit-Everyone_2015.pdf)
- **Critique of Gernsbacher:** the critic found no objective-measure studies of neurotypical K-12 learners reading same-language captions, and argues that cognitive-load theory predicts captions can hurt on complex material. [PRACTITIONER-OPINION, education writer] — [Learning and the Brain](https://www.learningandthebrain.com/blog/video-captions-benefit-everyone-an-investigation/)
- **Subtitle speed:** faster subtitles reduce comprehension and lead to more incomplete reading. [PEER-REVIEWED] — [Cambridge, Applied Psycholinguistics, "Why subtitle speed matters"](https://www-cambridge-org.ezproxyberklee.flo.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/A7DB0CBF5910353143738CF9C1721317/S0142716421000503a.pdf/why-subtitle-speed-matters-evidence-from-word-skipping-and-rereading.pdf)
- **Dynamic placement:** subtitles placed near the speaker were less disruptive than traditional bottom subtitles, "although not for all types of user". [PEER-REVIEWED] — [Dynamic subtitles: the user experience](https://typeset.io/pdf/dynamic-subtitles-the-user-experience-1ljhdbo7lt.pdf)
- **Hoyos on hooks:** the hook must be **visual and understandable with the sound off**. She sketches the first frame before writing words. On Reels she puts subtitles on every second because many viewers watch muted. [PRACTITIONER-OPINION] — [Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)
- **Safe zones:** no official organic safe-zone spec found this round. Third-party measurements disagree (already tabled in the kit). Instagram's grid now crops covers to 3:4: keep key cover content in the centre 1080×1440 of a 1080×1920 frame. [VENDOR blogs] — [Planoly](https://planoly.com/blog/guide-to-instagrams-new-vertical-grid); [Storrito](https://storrito.com/resources/why-instagrams-3-4-profile-grid-crop-still-frustrates-creators-2026/)

### Inferences
- **Confirms kit:** burn captions in on every Short. Don't justify it with "85% watch muted". Justify it with comprehension (Gernsbacher), Tamil-dialect and second-language viewers, and Reels muted viewing.
- **Kit nuance:** the kit cites "a review of 100+ studies found captions improve comprehension…for everyone". Soften "for everyone", because the critique stands. The benefit is strongest for second-language viewers, which matters for non-Tamil or diaspora viewers.
- Word-by-word kinetic captions are an untested genre convention. Since faster subtitle reading cuts comprehension, show **short phrases (2–4 words) timed to speech** rather than flashing single Tamil words. Tamil words are long, and agglutinative forms may not fit one per beat.
- Keyword highlighting (one coloured word per line in turmeric `#E3A72F`) is [PRACTITIONER-OPINION] only. It costs nothing, so A/B it.
- **Safe zones:** the kit's box (x 90–918, y 260–1470) sits entirely inside the 3:4 grid crop (y 240–1680), so the IG grid cover works if the opening question sits inside it. **Confirms kit.**

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or platform data on word-by-word or kinetic captions versus static, or on highlight colour.
- No current, platform-measured sound-off share for Shorts or Reels.
- **Tamil:** no research found on caption readability, reading speed or line length for Tamil or other Indic scripts in short video. Glyph-shaping risks are already in the kit. Reading-speed data for Tamil readers remains an open gap.

## Sound design

### Takeaway
There is little hard data on SFX or music and retention. The only quantitative audio evidence comes from TikTok-commissioned Kantar research, which measures ads and attitudes, not retention. Licensing rules are the firmer ground, and the kit already covers them.

### Cited Findings
- **Kantar for TikTok:** ads with sound on were rated more "fun" (+66% versus other platforms with sound off), and 73% of respondents said they would "stop and look" at TikTok ads with audio. [VENDOR] — [TikTok for Business](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/kantar-report-how-brands-are-making-noise-and-driving-impact-with-sound-on-tiktok)
- **YouTube Audio Library:** tracks "won't be claimed", there is an "attribution not required" filter, and the page is silent on use outside YouTube. [OFFICIAL, already in kit] — [YouTube Help 3376882](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3376882?hl=en)
- **Instagram logos and watermarks:** your own logo on a Reel is fine. Reels with **other apps'** logos or watermarks are not recommended in Explore or Reels. [OFFICIAL via Mosseri statement, secondary report] — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-clarifies-including-your-own-logo-on-a-reel-is-ok/730852)
- **Instagram originality (2025):** accounts posting mostly unoriginal content stop being recommended to non-followers. "Meaningfully enhanced" content is exempt. [OFFICIAL via secondary report] — [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/influence-zone-news/push-for-original-content-signals-bigger-reset-in-creator-economy-154828.html)

### Inferences
- **Confirms kit:** no licensed music, CC0 or self-made whoosh and sting, and a log of every audio asset.
- **Export clean masters per platform:** no YouTube Shorts watermark on the Reels upload, and no Gemini sparkle. Instagram suppresses other apps' marks **(inference from the logo rule)**.
- A **fixed, short verdict sting** works as an audio brand cue. That rests on sonic-branding opinion, not retention data [PRACTITIONER-OPINION].
- If a bed is used, keep it far below the VO, because Stable Volume compresses the mix. Reasonable practice is roughly −25 to −30 LUFS for the bed under a −14 VO [PRACTITIONER-OPINION, no source fetched].

### Gaps
- No study on whooshes, risers or silence against Shorts retention.
- No official VO-to-music level guidance from any platform.
- Instagram "original audio" naming and remix behaviour was not researched this round.

## Visual pacing and editing

### Takeaway
No frame-level, cut-counted breakdown of Zack D. Films exists publicly. What is documented: very short videos (about 31 s for a 253M-view Short), a question stated in the first second, an ending the moment the answer lands, and very high production cost ($1,000 per clip). Galloway's data ties success to watch time and "viewed vs swiped away", not to any cut rate. "Cut every 1–3 seconds" rules are folklore.

### Cited Findings
- **Zack D. Films, "How Stitches Work":** 31 s and 253M views. The needle is on skin in the first second, and the video ends "the moment it answers". [PRACTITIONER-OPINION analysis with reported numbers] — [Daniel Rusnok, Substack](https://danielrusnok.substack.com/p/how-a-faceless-channel-engineered)
- **Zack D. Films pays freelance animators** $1,000 per 20–45 s clip with about 8 days of work. [PRACTITIONER-DATA, job posting, already in kit] — [Quickdraw Animation](https://quickdrawanimation.ca/opportunities/job-postings/zack-d-films-animator-wanted)
- **Galloway and Gileta (April 2023):** 5,400 Shorts, 33 channels, 3.3B views. [PRACTITIONER-DATA] — [Galloway thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html)
  - Most Shorts ran 20–40 s, but longer ones averaged far more views. Shorts with AVD over 50 s averaged 4.1M views.
  - Shorts with **viewed-vs-swiped-away under 60% rarely did well**; the best sat at 70–90%.
  - Likes, comments and shares showed no strong link to performance.
  - Make the first second "really punchy… treat the intro like a thumbnail".
  - This was the 60-second-cap era.
- **"Viewed (vs. swiped away)"** is an official Shorts analytics metric. YouTube publishes no target threshold. [OFFICIAL] — [YouTube Help 12220281](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281)
- **Hoyos's numbers:** she cut 1 second from the end of one Short and retention rose from 83% to 88%, after which it "took off". [PRACTITIONER-DATA, single anecdote] — [Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)
  - Her benchmarks: YouTube Shorts about 34 s, slower and more story-driven; TikTok 10–20 s; aim for 90%+ retention and a scroll-through rate of about 70% or better (hers is about 85%).
  - Under 30 s, retention must be very high or the Short won't take off.
- **"Cuts per minute" figures** circulating online (about 50 for action, 22 for emotional, 14 for drama) are one creator's informal count, not Shorts-specific. [PRACTITIONER-OPINION] — search summary; source not fetched.

### Inferences
- The engine is the **question in the first frame and an end at the answer**, plus constant motion. It is not a cut rate. For stills, the kit's slow push-in or parallax on every non-animated shot already delivers "motion in every shot". **Confirms kit.**
- Change the visual at each new idea or sentence of the script, not on a timer. Galloway's and Hoyos's data reward removing dead time, not adding cuts.
- **Length:** with a 60% swipe floor and AVD rewarded, a 30–45 s Tamil explainer that holds attention is the sweet spot for a new channel. Galloway's "longer is better" comes from established channels, while Hoyos warns that short runtimes need very high retention.
- **Track "viewed vs swiped away" per Short** in `metrics-sheet.csv`, with a <60% red flag. Galloway's threshold is the only published benchmark.

### Gaps
- No frame-level shot or cut counts for Zack D. Films or similar animated channels. Measure 3–5 of them yourself (count cuts in an editor).
- No peer-reviewed study on zoom or punch-in frequency and short-video retention.
- Galloway's data predates 3-minute Shorts. YouTube extended Shorts to 3 minutes in October 2024, but that page was not fetched this round. Unknown whether his length finding still holds.

## Packaging: titles, first frame, covers, emoji, series branding

### Takeaway
No official source says Shorts titles drive feed distribution. Practitioners agree titles matter mainly in search, on the channel page and in suggested surfaces. The first frame is the real "thumbnail" in the feed: Hoyos sketches it first, and Galloway says treat it like one. Thumbnail frame selection exists in the mobile app. On Instagram the 3:4 grid crop governs the cover.

### Cited Findings
- **Shorts titles in the feed:** sources disagree on whether the title is visible in the swipe feed at all. All agree it matters in search and on channel pages. Practical length is about 20–40 characters, with a 100-character limit. [PRACTITIONER-OPINION] — [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-shorts-titles-thumbnails-matter); [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-titles/)
- **"#Shorts"** in the title: one analysis found no consistent advantage. [PRACTITIONER-DATA, unverified sample] — [Overseeros](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/should-you-put-shorts-in-youtube-title)
- **Zack D. Films titles** follow "a handful of words, exactly one emoji, every time". His description is empty: no keywords, hashtags or links. [PRACTITIONER-OPINION observation] — [Rusnok](https://danielrusnok.substack.com/p/how-a-faceless-channel-engineered)
- **Shorts thumbnail:** you can choose a frame in the YouTube mobile app during or after upload. Custom image upload is described as a gradual rollout, and sources conflict on it. A common workaround puts a designed frame inside the video and selects it. [VENDOR guides; official page not fetched] — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/es/blog/post/youtube-shorts-custom-thumbnails); [thumbnailtest](https://thumbnailtest.com/guides/cant-change-shorts-thumbnail/)
- **First frame:** the hook should be visual, work with sound off, and pass the "would this work as a long-form title and thumbnail?" test. Hoyos's hook is the hook plus a 2-line foreshadow, at about 3 s or less. [PRACTITIONER-OPINION] — [Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/)
- **Instagram grid:** the profile grid moved to 3:4 in 2025. Reel covers are 9:16, cropped to the centre 3:4 (about 1080×1440). You can pick a frame or upload a cover and reposition it. [VENDOR] — [Planoly](https://planoly.com/blog/guide-to-instagrams-new-vertical-grid); [Oktopost](https://www.oktopost.com/blog/instagram-grid-size-guide/)
- **Emoji in titles:** no data found beyond the Zack D. Films observation. [MYTH/UNVERIFIED for any performance claim]

### Inferences
- **Title job:** title for search and for channel-page browsing. Mixed Tamil and English keywords help ("Nerve" beside Tamil). One emoji is a cheap brand habit copied from Zack D. Films; it is not evidence-backed. **Confirms kit** on English keywords.
- **First frame = cover:** design frame 0 as the cover. That means the Tamil opening question in the safe box, Mani mid-reaction, and no black or fade-in frame. Then select that frame as the Shorts thumbnail and the Reels cover, so one asset serves YouTube, the IG grid (inside the 3:4 crop) and the feed.
- **Series branding:** the kit's recurring cast and fixed verdict sting match the documented pattern (the muthupandi series, Zack D. Films' consistent style). No data isolates intro stings. Keep any sting under 1 s and never before the hook, because Galloway's swipe-floor data punishes a slow first second.

### Gaps
- No official YouTube statement on whether title text affects Shorts feed ranking.
- No controlled data on emoji, title length or on-screen title text for Shorts.
- No official Instagram help page fetched on cover selection or grid crop.
- No data on Instagram's 2025 originality rules applied to AI-animated faceless accounts specifically.
