# Cross-platform short-form formats (Reels / TikTok / X / FB Reels → YouTube Shorts), 2024–2026

Scope note: ~16 search/fetch calls. Platform-policy facts (originality, lengths, engagement bait, misinformation) are well sourced. "Viral format" and "hook" data is weak: almost all hook/retention numbers online come from vendor blogs with no primary dataset. Those are flagged. Format catalogue items without a source are in Inferences, not Cited Findings.

## 1. Which recurring formats dominate, and which fit a faceless animated explainer?

### Takeaway
No reliable dataset ranks formats (myth-vs-fact, POV, "part 2", quiz, etc.) against each other; the evidence is about mechanics (watch time, sends, originality), not format labels. The strongest verifiable precedent for a faceless animated explainer is Zack D. Films (~28–29M subscribers, ~72–81B views, almost all Shorts), and a myth-busting "curiosity arc" structure is described by practitioners but not measured.

### Cited Findings
- Zack D. Films (@zackdfilms): ~29.3M subscribers as of 2026-10-21, +800K subs between 2026-09-24 and 2026-10-21, ~81.0B total views (vidIQ) — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@zackdfilms/). Other trackers differ: 27.6M subs / 71.8B views and an estimate that ~100% of views come from Shorts — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/channel/zackdfilms); 28M subs — [Social Blade](https://socialblade.com/youtube/handle/zackdfilmsbro). Treat as range, not exact.
- Zack D. Films' success is attributed (third-party, opinion) to 3D animated shorts, pacing, edits and storytelling; one source claims 50+ animators. No official production account found — [Recapio digest](https://recapio.com/digest/i-found-the-secret-website-to-make-zack-d-films-by-boss-media-tech); [Guidesify](https://opinion.guidesify.com/zack-d-films-the-man-who-explains-various-random-interesting-topics-across-the-internet/). Claims that the style can be cloned with free AI tools at "5–10 shorts a day" are promotional and unverified — same Recapio source.
- Myth-busting structure (opinion, practitioner guide): the best myth-busting videos follow a curiosity arc — notice the myth, question it aloud, walk through evidence, land on a conclusion the viewer "discovers alongside" the creator; comments expressing surprise/sharing can beat higher-reach clips dismissed as ads. Not a controlled study — [Influencers-Time](https://www.influencers-time.com/myth-busting-creator-videos-that-build-trust-not-suspicion/).
- Socialinsider 2025 Instagram benchmarks (brand accounts): Reels avg engagement rate 0.50% per post vs images 0.45%; carousels led H1 2025 at 0.55% — [Socialinsider 2025 Instagram benchmarks](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-hashtag-study/); [PDF](https://www.socialinsider.io/data-geeks/instagram_benchmarks_2025.pdf). Note: brand data, not creators.

- Faceless-Shorts topic categories repeatedly named by niche guides (tool-vendor opinion, no ranked view data): history facts, did-you-know science, nature/wildlife, psychology facts, motivation, finance tips, recipes; quick science/history/nature facts called high-potential for Shorts — [Eliro](https://eliro.pro/blog/faceless-youtube-shorts-niches-2026); [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/vi/blog/post/youtube-channel-ideas-without-showing-your-face); [Fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-shorts-ai-faceless-niche-2026).
- Psychology/"I didn't know that" facts are pitched as the main Shorts engagement driver (opinion) — [Fluxnote facts guide](https://fluxnote.io/guides/faceless-youtube-facts-channel-guide).
- Mass-produced "AI slop" (generic stock footage, robotic TTS, no editorial judgment) is reported to get demonetised or buried; a distinctive editorial angle matters more than the format — [Eliro](https://eliro.pro/blog/faceless-youtube-shorts-niches-2026). Consistent with platform originality rules in §5.

### Inferences
- **Format catalogue judged on its own merit for a faceless animated Tamil channel (any topic):**

  | Format | Faceless-animated fit | Transfers to Shorts? | Notes |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Animated explainer / "what happens if…" story (Zack D. Films model) | Excellent — the proven faceless template | Yes, native | Biggest verifiable precedent; production-heavy per video |
  | Did-you-know / fact drop (science, body, history, nature, psychology) | Excellent | Yes | Most-named faceless niches; easy to batch; risk of sameness → needs a voice/character |
  | Myth vs fact / "is it true?" with verdict | Excellent | Yes | Built-in open loop to the verdict; comment-friendly |
  | "You've been doing X wrong" | Good | Yes | A hook framing, works over many topics |
  | Quiz / guess-the-answer (pause before reveal) | Excellent | Yes | Drives comments; reveal = retention anchor |
  | "Things only X people understand" / identity | Good (needs relatable visuals) | Yes | Strong DM/WhatsApp share driver (sends) |
  | Rating / ranking / tier list ("ranking X from worst to best") | Good | Yes | Countdown structure holds watch time; debate in comments |
  | Storytime (history, folklore, true events, mysteries) | Good via narrator + animation | Yes | Longer (60–90s+); retention depends on story tension |
  | POV | Weak–medium | Yes, but face/acting-driven | Works only with a strong recurring animated character |
  | Before/after, transformation | Weak for explainer | Yes | Fits visual-change topics only (how a city/object changed over time) |
  | Part 1/2/3 serials | Medium | Partly | Each Short is served alone; every part must stand alone |
  | Trending-audio memes / lip-sync / dance trends | Poor | Platform-specific (TikTok/Reels) | Depends on licensed trending sound; low fit for explainer |
  | Duets/stitches/reaction | Poor | TikTok/Reels-specific | Requires other creators' clips; originality risk on Meta |
  | Text-on-screen X-style hot takes | Medium | Partly | X video favours commentary/news clips; no data found |

- The two concept series map onto this table (myth vs fact + quiz; identity + rating), but the table stands independent of them.

- Concept-specific fit (original two series, judgement, not data):
  - **Myth vs fact / "is it true?" with verdict** — core fit; matches the curiosity arc above and is fully faceless-compatible. Transfers to all four platforms.
  - **"You've been doing X wrong"** — fits as an alternate hook framing of the same episode ("Saapittu udane kuliikka koodadhu — paati sonnadhu sariya?"). Transfers.
  - **Quiz / guess-the-verdict** (pause before reveal: "unmai / paadhi unmai / poi — comment-la sollunga") — fits; drives comments. Transfers.
  - **"Things only X people understand" / identity** — best fit for the weekly city-identity series ("Madurai kaaranga mattum purinjikka mudiyum"). Identity content is shareable to the in-group via DM/WhatsApp, which is exactly the "sends" signal Instagram rewards (see §4). Transfers.
  - **"Rating X"** — fits city series (rating city foods/phrases), mild fit for myths (a "paati meter"). Transfers.
  - **Part 1/2/3 serials** — usable sparingly (e.g., multi-part myth on one topic), but each Short is served alone in the feed, so every part must stand on its own.
  - **POV / storytime / before-after** — POV and storytime are face/voice-persona driven; workable only through a recurring animated character (Paati as narrator). Before/after has little fit for myths.
- The verdict ending (true / half-true / false) is a recurring, recognisable device in itself — functionally a catchphrase and a branded end card.

### Gaps
- No primary dataset comparing viral rates across format types (myth-vs-fact, POV, quiz, "part 2"). Searches returned only generic advice and low-quality/auto-generated pages.
- No verified Tamil-language example of a myth-busting Shorts channel with numbers was found in this pass.

## 2. What hook formulas (first line / first frame) are documented to work?

### Takeaway
Every source agrees the first 1–3 seconds decide the swipe, but specific thresholds ("70–85% 3-second retention = 2.2x views") are repeated across vendor blogs without a primary source and should be treated as unverified. Platforms expose the real metric directly: YouTube's "Viewed vs. swiped away" (Shorts) and TikTok's retention curve.

### Cited Findings
- Vendor blogs put a "good" 3-second retention at roughly 50–85% (inconsistent); the widely repeated "70–85% retention yields 2.2x more views" has no named primary source — [Opus Clip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/tiktok-length-format-retention-data); [autoclip.dev](https://autoclip.dev/blog/viral-clip-anatomy-3-second-hook-data); [Virlo](https://virlo.ai/blog/why-the-first-few-seconds-matter). Unverified.
- Claimed scroll-decision windows conflict (0.9s / 1.5s / 1.7s) across blogs — same sources above. Unverified.
- Claim: 2–3 caption changes in the first 3 seconds outperform one static caption or none; and starting the hook in the first second lifted 2–3s retention 63%→81%. No dataset cited — [autoclip.dev](https://autoclip.dev/blog/viral-clip-anatomy-3-second-hook-data). Opinion-grade.
- YouTube Studio → Analytics → Content → Shorts shows "Viewed vs. swiped away"; third-party guides suggest aiming for ~75–80% viewed, but there is no official threshold — [ContentStudio](https://contentstudio.io/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics-guide); [Creator Essentials glossary](https://www.creatoressentials.com/glossary/viewed-vs-swiped-away/); [Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics-guide/).
- TikTok Analytics shows a per-video retention curve including % remaining at 3 seconds — [Virlo](https://virlo.ai/blog/why-the-first-few-seconds-matter).
- Mosseri (Jan 2025, via Social Media Today): top Instagram ranking signals are watch time, likes and sends; creators should check average watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-2025/738034/).

### Inferences
- General hook formulas (any topic, practitioner patterns, not measured): question/claim in frame 1 with on-screen text; "what happens if…"; "X% of people don't know…"; second-person "you're doing X wrong"; stakes/countdown ("3 things… #1 will shock you" style, minus clickbait); in-media-res visual (start mid-action, no intro); open loop to a reveal at the end.
- Myth-series-specific examples: (a) claim-as-question in frame 1 + text on screen ("Raathiri nagam vettakoodadhu — yen?"); (b) direct second-person challenge ("Neenga ithai daily pannringa…"); (c) stakes/surprise ("Paati sonnadhu 100% correct — science proof"); (d) open loop to the verdict ("Last-la verdict paarunga"). Put the myth itself in the first spoken line and as on-screen text in frame 1 — never a logo intro.
- Since the hook metric is directly observable on YouTube (viewed vs swiped) and TikTok, A/B the first line across episodes rather than trusting blog thresholds. Instagram Trial Reels (see §5) is a free hook-testing tool.

### Gaps
- No TikTok Creator Academy or YouTube primary statement giving a numeric 3-second benchmark was found.
- No hook data specific to Tamil / Indian-language audiences.

## 3. How do successful creators build series and recurring characters/catchphrases?

### Takeaway
Hard evidence is thin; the documented platform lever is consistency of original posting (Instagram's own guidance). Recurring characters/catchphrases are an observed pattern (e.g., Zack D. Films' consistent visual style) but no study quantifies follow-rate lift.

### Cited Findings
- Instagram: "One of the most effective ways to grow is to post original content consistently"; recommendations no longer favour large accounts, so creators of all sizes have equal chance to reach new audiences — [Instagram Creators blog, "Finding success on reels in 2025"](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/the-latest-with-instagram?).
- vidIQ: Shorts don't hurt a channel and can feed long-form via related links — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/12-youtube-myths-distract-getting-views). Opinion from a tool vendor.
- Zack D. Films' recognisable house style (3D look, pacing, storytelling) is credited for its growth — [Recapio digest](https://recapio.com/digest/i-found-the-secret-website-to-make-zack-d-films-by-boss-media-tech). Opinion.

### Inferences
- Series devices for this channel: a fixed opener line ("Paati sonnadhu unmaiya?"), a recurring animated Paati character, a fixed 3-state verdict stamp (unmai / paadhi unmai / poi) with consistent colour and sound sting, and a fixed weekly slot for the city series. These double as brand recognition across cross-posts.
- Use YouTube's related-video link / playlists to chain episodes; on Instagram, pin a "start here" Reel.

### Gaps
- No data found on "part 2" series retention or follow-conversion; no data on catchphrase/character effects.

## 4. What drives comments and shares? What evidence exists?

### Takeaway
Instagram has repeatedly named sends (DM shares) per reach as a top ranking signal (2024–2025); content that people forward to a specific person (identity, "this is so you", family arguments) is the share mechanism. Explicitly asking for tags/shares/votes is demoted on Facebook as "engagement bait" (policy since Dec 2017; current status not reconfirmed).

### Cited Findings
- Mosseri (2024): send rate matters more than raw send count — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-chief-post-share-rates-key-driver-reach/716540/).
- Mosseri (Jan 2025): top three signals are watch time, likes, sends — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-2025/738034/). Secondary claims that sends are "the single most powerful signal for reaching non-followers" are third-party characterisations — [Lindsey Gamble](https://www.lindseygamble.com/blog/unpacking-instagrams-focus-on-sends-per-reach-a-timeline-why-it-matters-and-tips-for-creators-brands); [Search Engine Journal](https://searchenginejournal.com/instagram-algorithm-shift-why-sends-matter-more-than-ever/521389).
- Instagram does not give a native "sends per reach" metric; creators compute it manually — [InfluencerMarketingHub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/instagram-sends-per-reach-playbook/).
- Facebook "engagement bait" (Dec 2017): demotes posts using vote-bait, react-bait, share-bait, tag-bait ("tag a friend") and comment-bait; ML model trained on hundreds of thousands of posts; Pages that repeat it get larger reach drops; posts are demoted, not removed — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/12/18/facebook-is-clamping-down-on-posts-that-shamelessly-beg-for-your-engagement/?ncid=rss); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/facebook-announces-news-feed-update-to-crack-down-on-engagement-baiting-p/513336). **Pre-2024 source** — still the published Meta stance as far as found, but 2026 status not reconfirmed.

### Inferences
- Comment drivers that avoid "bait" wording: ask for genuine information, not a reaction ("Unga paati enna solluvaanga?" / "Unga ooru-la ithu ennanu solluvaanga?"), and pre-verdict guessing. Avoid "tag your friend / share if you agree / comment YES".
- Share driver: the verdict itself — a "false" verdict on a rule everyone's family enforces is the thing people forward to family WhatsApp groups / DM. Half-true verdicts invite debate in comments (controversy-lite) without misinformation risk.
- City-identity series is a natural "sends" engine (in-group pride/teasing).

### Gaps
- No quantitative study found linking comment-prompt wording to comment rate; no YouTube-specific engagement-bait policy found for Shorts in this pass.

## 5. Platform differences: length, captions, audio, watermarks, originality, Facebook Reels in India

### Takeaway
All four main surfaces now allow ~3-minute shorts (YouTube Oct 2024, Instagram Jan 2025), but brand benchmarks still peak well under that (IG Reels 45–60s best engagement in Socialinsider 2026 data). Both Instagram (Apr 2024) and Facebook (Jul 2025) actively demote non-original/duplicate content and favour the original; another app's watermark is widely reported (not officially confirmed in what was found) to be down-ranked — so upload clean masters to each platform rather than re-sharing watermarked exports.

### Cited Findings
- **YouTube Shorts length**: since 15 Oct 2024, square/vertical uploads up to 3 minutes count as Shorts (announced 3 Oct 2024) — [Engadget](https://engadget.com/entertainment/youtube/youtube-shorts-can-now-run-up-to-three-minutes-160002081.html); [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2024/10/04/youtube-shorts-expands-video-length-to-three-minutes/); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-expands-3-minute-shorts-to-all-users/736967/).
- **YouTube metrics**: Shorts revenue share is paid on "engaged views" — [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/youtube-shorts-view-count-update-wins-over-brands-but-creators-arent-sold/). Definitions of engaged views vary across third-party glossaries; check YouTube Help.
- **Instagram length**: Jan 2025, Reels up to 3 minutes; previously only Reels ≤90s were eligible for recommendation to non-followers; now ≤3 min can be recommended on Reels tab/Explore — [Instagram Creators blog](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/the-latest-with-instagram?); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-officially-expands-reels-length-3-minutes/737766/). Mosseri: "We still are focused on short form video over long form" — [Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/instagram-reels-tips-3-minutes-unfiltered-nyc/742335/).
- **IG Reels length data (brands, 2026)**: 45–60s Reels had the highest engagement rate (0.35%); >3 min dropped to 0.15% — [Socialinsider](https://socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-length/). Vendor aggregate; brand accounts.
- **Facebook Reels length**: Socialinsider suggests 120–180s for Facebook; a secondary source says 90–120s performs best — [Socialinsider video stats](https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/social-media-video-statistics). Conflicting; weak.
- **Instagram originality (Apr 30, 2024)**: if two or more identical pieces of content are found, only the original will be recommended; reposts get a label linking to the original creator; aggregators/large accounts had been getting outsized reach — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/instagrams-algorithm-overhaul-will-reward-original-content-and-penalize-aggregators-130018977.html); [Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/apps-software/instagrams-new-ranking-system-prioritizes-original-content). Original 2022 Mosseri framing: originality predicted from signals incl. whether Instagram has seen the content before — [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90744109/instagram-tiktok-algorithm-adam-mosseri) (**2022, older**). 2026 expansion of originality rules to photos/carousels and aggregator blocking — [PetaPixel, Apr 2026](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/).
- **Watermarks**: third-party sources say Reels carrying another app's (e.g., TikTok) watermark are de-prioritised — [dot.la](https://dot.la/instagram-tiktok-content-2657200198.html). Not confirmed in an official Instagram statement in this pass. Instagram's own Edits app advertises "export without a watermark" — [Instagram Creators blog](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/the-latest-with-instagram?).
- **Facebook unoriginal-content crackdown (Jul 2025)**: accounts that repeatedly reuse others' content without meaningful changes lose monetisation for a period and get reduced distribution on everything they post; duplicates' reach cut in favour of original; commentary/reaction/trend participation with a unique take exempt; ~500K accounts penalised for spam/fake engagement and ~10M impersonator profiles removed in H1 2025 (Meta figures via press) — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/facebook-cracking-down-unoriginal-content-ai-replicas/753001/); [Bitdefender](https://www.bitdefender.com/en-us/blog/hotforsecurity/facebooks-crackdown-on-reposted-content-what-creators-need-to-know); [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/sci-tech/2025/07/18/posting-on-facebook-here-s-why-your-old-strategy-might-not-work-anymore.html).
- **Trial Reels**: lets creators test a Reel on non-followers first, then share to followers if it performs — [Instagram Creators blog](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/the-latest-with-instagram?). Meta India: with Trial Reels, 40% of creators shared more and 80% of those saw higher non-follower reach — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/meta-rolls-out-india-specific-instagram-reels-tools-and-new-regional-fonts-for-edits-app-84992.htm) (Meta-reported).
- **Audio**: audio layering on Instagram; a unique audio mix is attributed to the creator and can be remixed by others — [Instagram Creators blog](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/the-latest-with-instagram?). Claims that trending audio gives +29% or +40% reach are unsourced — [Zebracat](https://www.zebracat.ai/post/instagram-reels-statistics). Unverified.
- **Tamil-specific Meta tools**: Meta AI reel translation (voice dubbing + lip-sync) expanded to Tamil, Bengali, Telugu, Kannada, Marathi on Instagram and Facebook (announced Nov 2025, live ~Jan 2026); Edits got Indian-script fonts (Devanagari, Bengali-Assamese at launch) — [about.fb.com](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/11/instagram-empowers-creators-to-go-global-with-local-voice-translations-and-fonts/amp/); [India TV](https://www.indiatvnews.com/technology/news/instagram-expands-meta-ai-reel-translations-to-bengali-tamil-telugu-kannada-and-marathi-2026-01-17-1026257); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-adds-more-languages-to-ai-translations-for-reels/810019/).
- Regional language demand (opinion piece by Meta India head): 98% of Indian internet users access Indic-language content; Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam among most popular; ~57% of urban users prefer regional languages — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/magazine/sandhya-devanathan-writes-rise-of-regional-creators-will-drive-next-gen-creativity). Not Facebook-Reels-specific.

### Inferences
- Cross-posting rule: export one clean, watermark-free master from the editor (not a downloaded YouTube/TikTok copy), upload natively to each platform under the same account that owns it. Because the channel owns the original, duplicate-detection should favour it, but this is not guaranteed if the first-indexed copy is elsewhere — publish the "home" platform first.
- Length: aim for 30–60s for the myth episodes so the same cut works everywhere; city-series can run longer (60–90s) on YouTube/Facebook.
- On-screen text: burn in large Tamil captions (most viewers are sound-off and Tamil script also anchors the content as original); keep critical text out of bottom ~20% and right edge where UI overlays sit.
- Audio: use original voiceover + own music bed (attributed to you, reusable as a remixable sound) rather than depending on trending tracks, which can be licence-limited differently per platform.
- Meta AI dubbing could re-voice Tamil episodes into Telugu/Kannada/Hindi for reach, but quality for a voice-character format is untested.

### Gaps
- No verified daily-user figures for Facebook Reels in India or Tamil Nadu.
- No official Instagram/Meta statement found that explicitly names "watermarks" as a ranking demotion (only third-party).
- X (Twitter) video: no 2024–2026 data found in this pass on format performance; omitted rather than guessed.
- TikTok is banned in India (since 2020, general knowledge) — TikTok findings are relevant only as format inspiration, not as a distribution channel for this audience.

## 6. What did not work / backfired?

### Takeaway
The documented backfires are (a) engagement-bait wording on Facebook, (b) reposting/aggregating others' content (Instagram 2024, Facebook 2025 — loss of reach and monetisation), and (c) health claims: YouTube's medical misinformation policy covers promoting harmful remedies, with warning → strike → termination escalation — directly relevant to a show that judges household health rules.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube medical misinformation policy has three buckets (prevention, treatment, denial); "treatment" covers promoting harmful remedies/substances (examples: MMS, black salve, high-grade hydrogen peroxide); educational/documentary/scientific context can be allowed but is not a pass to promote misinformation — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13813322?hl=en); [YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/a-long-term-vision-for-medical-misinformation-policies/).
- Enforcement: first violation usually a warning + policy training; another violation within 90 days of training completion → strike; repeated strikes can lead to termination — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9891785); [Skeptics NZ, 2024](https://articles.skeptics.nz/2024/06/24/in-the-youtube-naughty-corner).
- Facebook engagement-bait demotion — see §4 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/12/18/facebook-is-clamping-down-on-posts-that-shamelessly-beg-for-your-engagement/?ncid=rss), 2017).
- Facebook unoriginal-content penalties (reduced distribution on all posts, monetisation loss) — see §5 ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/facebook-cracking-down-unoriginal-content-ai-replicas/753001/)).
- Instagram 2026: aggregator accounts blocked from recommendations unless mostly original; watermark + caption credit is reportedly not a "meaningful change" (third-party wording, unconfirmed) — [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/).

### Inferences
- Biggest risk for "Paati sonnadhu unmaiya?" is a "true" verdict on a remedy that contradicts health-authority guidance (e.g., treating a disease with a home remedy). Restrict verdicts on medical-treatment myths to what WHO/ICMR/peer-reviewed sources support, cite on screen, and frame half-true carefully. Lifestyle/household myths (cutting nails at night, sweeping after dusk) are low-risk.
- AI-generated "faceless" content that mass-produces near-identical videos risks being classed as unoriginal/repetitive (Facebook 2025 explicitly targets "AI replicas" per SMT headline) — keep scripts, research and verdicts genuinely original.

### Gaps
- No YouTube Shorts-specific case of a home-remedy creator receiving a strike was found.
- YouTube's July 2025 "inauthentic/mass-produced content" monetisation update was referenced in passing ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/after-youtube-meta-crackdowns-on-unoriginal-content-removes-10-million-fake-facebook-profiles-74532.htm)) but not fetched or verified here.
