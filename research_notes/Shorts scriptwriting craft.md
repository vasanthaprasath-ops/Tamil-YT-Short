# Shorts scriptwriting craft: what proven creators do (2026-11-13)

Most of these sources are secondary write-ups: interview summaries and creator blogs. Treat the rules as practitioner craft, not measured science. They agree with each other, and with our psychology notes (`Short video formats that work/psychology_attention_sharing.md`).

Evidence grades for these rules are in `reports/Short form video craft playbook.md`. All rules below are PRACTITIONER level. Rules 8 and 10 have creator numbers behind them; none has a controlled test.

## Rules

1. **One subject, one question.** A hook names one topic and puts the same question in every viewer's head. If the hook is vague, each viewer guesses a different subject and leaves. Kallaway, via [SozAI transcript](https://sozai.app/transcript/create-killer-hook-impossible-skip/) and [Recapio](https://recapio.com/digest/how-to-create-a-killer-hook-impossible-to-skip-by-kallaway).
2. **The three hooks must match.** What's on screen, what's said and the on-screen text all point at the same thing. Final audit: is the subject clear? Is the question clear? Are all three aligned? Would I watch the second sentence? Kallaway (same sources).
3. **The hook is under 3 seconds, works on mute, and uses very simple words.** Jenny Hoyos checks her scripts with a readability tool and aims for a 5th-grade reading level or below. Her test: would this work as a long-form title and thumbnail? [Creator Science podcast](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/). Readability tools are English-only. For Tamil, use spoken colloquial words and short clauses, and explain any technical term in one breath.
4. **Foreshadow right after the hook.** Within about 3 s, say what's coming, what the conflict is and what the viewer gets by the end. [Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/); [Marketing Examined](https://marketing-examined.beehiiv.com/p/jenny-hoyos-shortform-video-playbook).
5. **Give the viewer a visible mechanism.** For example, three clues or three steps, so they know how far they are from the payoff. Hoyos (same).
6. **Build the body from "but" and "therefore", never "and then".** Every beat changes the situation. This is Trey Parker and Matt Stone's rule, which Hoyos and Kallaway use for Shorts. [Matt Rickard](https://cdn.matt-rickard.com/but-therefore); [Perell](https://perell.com/note/but-therefore-rule/).
7. **Meet the expectation, then add a twist.** End on the biggest emotion: leave viewers "satisfied, but surprised". Hoyos, [Marketing Examined](https://marketing-examined.beehiiv.com/p/jenny-hoyos-shortform-video-playbook).
8. **End the moment the promise is delivered.** Zack D. Films' "How Stitches Work" (31 s, 253M views) asks one question and stops when it answers it. Hoyos: "Whatever you say you're gonna do, you end it right after you do it." Cutting one second from an ending raised her retention from 83% to 88%. [Rusnok breakdown](https://danielrusnok.substack.com/p/how-a-faceless-channel-engineered); [Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/).
9. **Write the hook and the last line first.** Then fill in the middle. Hoyos (same).
10. **Length.** Hoyos' best Shorts average about 34 s, and she found 10–20 s worked better on TikTok. Galloway's study of 5,400 Shorts found a clear link between length and views: Shorts with average view duration above 50 s averaged 4.1M views. That is correlational, from established channels in the 60-second era. Default to 30–45 s and go longer only when retention stays flat. [Thread Reader](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html); [Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/).
11. **Set the right expectation in the first seconds** and re-engage the viewer regularly. This comes from the reported MrBeast production guide, whose authenticity is unconfirmed. [Creator Handbook](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/leaked-document-allegedly-reveals-mrbeasts-secrets-to-youtube-success-the-key-takeaways/). This guide is about long-form and its authenticity is unconfirmed. Ignore any 're-hook every N seconds' number. The rule is that every line changes the situation.
12. **Captions are part of the script.** Break lines for emphasis and highlight keywords. [Toptal](https://www.toptal.com/creator/post/how-to-write-a-youtube-script). (Practitioner opinion. Show 2–4-word phrases timed to speech; keyword highlighting is untested.)
13. **No spoken subscribe ask by default.** Spoken calls to action show as retention dips, and Zack D. Films uses none. Earn the follow with the recurring stamp, Paati and the series name.

## Added 2026-11-14: Kallaway, "Short-Form Storytelling Principles"

Source: a 5-page excerpt from Kallaway's paid Short Form Academy (a free lead magnet; local copy at `D:\Shorts\Short-Form Storytelling Principles.pdf`, not stored in this repo). It is one practitioner's teaching, written to sell a course, and gives no data. The numbers in it ("5 seconds", "6th grade") are rules of thumb. Where research backs a point, the grade says so.

14. **Contrast drives curiosity.** Map the viewer's common belief, then show the true reality that breaks it; the bigger the gap, the stronger the pull. Kallaway's own example claim (blueberry sauce beats sunscreen) is false: **for us the contrarian reality must be true and sourced, never invented for the gap.** *Grade: RESEARCH-backed in spirit (Loewenstein's information-gap theory of curiosity).*
15. **The leash: prove a bold claim fast.** The more outlandish the claim, and the more sure viewers are of their belief, the less time you get. Follow a surprising hook with one quick proof or plain example within ~5 s. Viewers who care about the topic and are unsure of their belief give you the longest leash. *Grade: PRACTITIONER opinion; plausible.* Ep 02 v10's hook "திட்ட வேண்டியது Dutch-அ!" needs its proof ("300 வருஷ பழைய map") right after it, which it has.
16. **Value signal by ~5 s; value density all the way.** For storytelling, value means "something interesting worth hearing". Count how often something unexpected happens; cut any line the viewer doesn't need. *Grade: PRACTITIONER; matches our surprise-by-3 s rule and Hoyos' "end when delivered".*
17. **Repeaters: say the key point twice.** Once short (with the term if needed), then once stupid-simple with a metaphor. Ep 02's card does this: "இது கோலம் மாதிரி தான். புள்ளி வெச்சது Dutch. கோலம் போட்டது French." **Repeat the fact, never the myth**: repetition makes any statement feel truer, including a myth. *Grade: RESEARCH-backed (illusory-truth effect; processing fluency).*
18. **Two levels simpler, two levels more interesting.** Reframe the core idea in plain words and active voice; one concept per sentence; define any term in one breath. *Grade: RESEARCH-backed (processing fluency: easy-to-process statements are understood and trusted more).*
19. **Walkaway value.** Can the viewer repeat the fact to someone after one watch? For us: could they tell it in the family WhatsApp group in one sentence? That is our share driver. *Grade: PRACTITIONER; fits our sharing research (practical value + face-saving correction).*
20. **Anticipation with a non-obvious reveal** is the strongest tactic: tease a condition so viewers wait for the answer, and the answer must contrast with what they guessed. *Grade: PRACTITIONER; already our foreshadow + mystery beat.*
21. **Pick one target emotion per video** and choose the words, visuals and sounds for it. Kallaway says fear and shock get the most shares. Research says high-arousal emotions (awe, surprise, anxiety, anger) get shared and sadness less (Berger & Milkman 2012), so awe and surprise belong on that list too. **Never manufacture fear**, especially on health topics. *Grade: RESEARCH-backed for arousal; "fear and shock most" is overstated.*
22. **Rhythm: mostly short sentences, but not all the same length.** Same-length lines drone; mix short punches (about 1–1.5 s) with medium lines, and keep run-ons out, because listeners lose the start of a long sentence. *Grade: rhythm is PRACTITIONER; the short-sentence part is RESEARCH-backed (working memory holds only a few chunks).*
23. **Energy carries through the screen.** A calm or monotone voice needs extra push in delivery. For our ElevenLabs voice (Prashant V, "Calm"), direct energy in the script, and test expressive settings on **one** take at the start of a new episode, never by re-recording a locked script. *Grade: PRACTITIONER.* Ep 02: Vasanth found natural speed slow and chose 1.08×.

## Added 2026-11-14: Kallaway Tech "Top Short-Form Video Collections" (names only)

Source: a one-page sales sheet (`D:\Shorts\Copy of Top Short-Form Video Collections (105 Collections).pdf`). The 105 analysed collections are behind a paid sign-up; the sheet shows only the four category groups and a dozen collection names. **There is no evidence or analysis in it**, so the list below is an idea menu, not a rule. *Grade: UNVERIFIED (labels only).*

- **Four ways to look at any Short:** its *visual hook* (what moves in frame 0), its *signature series* (the recurring segment people come back for), its *editing style*, and its *format*. Our channel already has a signature series (UNMAI / PAADHI UNMAI / POI stamp, the city "yen?" series); keep it identical every episode so it becomes the reason to subscribe.
- **Visual-hook menu for frame 0** (pick one per Short and don't repeat it in back-to-back episodes, so the channel never looks templated):
  - **Impending disaster:** something is about to go wrong to Mani (the leg about to buckle, the scooter heading for the same wrong corner).
  - **A vs B graphic:** two things side by side, one about to win (ep 02's temple town vs ruler town).
  - **Crash zoom onto the subject:** a fast push to the face or object on the first word (ours: the punch-in, used only on 1–2 beats).
  - **Camera whip:** a fast blurred pan into the scene (ours: at most one per film).
  - **Beat-matched switch:** the picture changes exactly on a music beat (ours: one tempo grid per episode).
- **Formats seen there that fit us:** breakdowns/explainers (our core), A-vs-B comparisons (good for city and "Paati vs science" episodes), and case studies. Skip "car yap", "about me" and challenge formats, which need a real face.

## Added 2026-11-14: "Viral content" (borrow a proven idea from another market)

Source: a two-page creator handout (`C:\Users\vasan\Downloads\Viral content.pdf` on the current laptop) on taking ideas that went viral in the US and remaking them for India. Half of it is a how-to for a scraping tool and two affiliate links. It gives **no data**. *Grade: PRACTITIONER opinion; the outlier idea matches what `/yt-viral` and our Zack D. Films teardown already do.*

**What is worth keeping: a way to find topics**
24. **Hunt outliers, not big channels.** A video is a signal when it did far better than its own channel normally does: views 10× the follower count, or several times the channel's own median. Pick 3–5 creators who do this **repeatedly**, never one lucky video. *We already measure this as "multiple over the channel's own median" (`/yt-viral`, `/ig-viral`).*
25. **Tear down each outlier with the same six questions**, then look across them:
    1. **Hook:** the exact opening line, its type (bold claim, question, curiosity gap…), why it stops the scroll.
    2. **Purpose:** educate, entertain, build trust or sell; what the viewer walks away with.
    3. **Structure:** beat by beat (hook → tension → payoff).
    4. **Key words:** the words that carry the emotion.
    5. **Psychology:** curiosity, relatability, identity, social proof, fear of missing out, controversy.
    6. **Patterns:** what the best ones share.
26. **Adapt the idea, three ways:** change the **angle** (put it in a Tamil home, street or family), change the **focus** (who is it for now, and what is their problem), and **raise the tension** only where it is true (higher stakes, before/after, contrast). Then write a new hook in spoken Tamil.

**Our limits on this method**
- **Borrow the idea and the structure, never the script, footage or audio.** Translating someone's video line by line is reused content (a demonetisation risk) and copying.
- **Re-source every fact.** A viral video is not a source; the adapted Short still needs its two credible sources and passes the topic filter (body, house, street, family memory).
- **No fear or money stakes invented for tension.** "Add higher stakes" applies only to stakes the sources support.
- **Don't run the Instagram scraper it recommends.** Scraping Instagram breaks its terms and risks the account. Collect by hand at human speed (`/ig-viral`), or read public YouTube titles and view counts (`/yt-viral`, or yt-dlp for titles and counts only). Never download others' videos.
- The two tools it links (Sandcastles, 1of10) are paid affiliate links; not needed.

Little for us; see "Not taken".

**Where the answer goes**
27. **The answer line sits in the last third.** Retention falls when the line after the hook gives the answer away, because nothing is left to wait for. The hook may state the surprise (*what*: "நல்ல sign!"); the *why* waits. Before locking a script, mark the line that answers the hook. If it is in the first two thirds, move it later and put a question where it was. *Grade: PRACTITIONER; consistent with information-gap curiosity (rule 14) and with our reference channel (mechanism at ~60 %, payoff at the end).* Ep 01 passes: the analogy starts at 64 % and the answer ("டிங் டிங் டிங் தான்… ஊசி குத்துறது!") lands at 33.6 s of 40.9 s, which is 82 %.
28. **One rehook in the middle, at about 40–55 %.** Under 45 s, exactly one; a second only past 45 s. For us it is the mystery beat. It must name this video's next thing, never "கடைசி வரைக்கும் பாருங்க" (stay till the end). Keep it to about 2.5 s spoken. It replaces any "rehook every N seconds" idea (rule 11). Ep 01's mystery line ("சரி, எழுந்ததும் ஏன் ஊசி குத்துது?") starts at 24.4 s, which is 60 %, a little late; its joke at 50 % helps. Next episode: aim the mystery at 45–55 %.
   Rehook lines in spoken Tamil (fill the [blank], pick one per video, don't repeat the same one in back-to-back episodes):
   - **Turn** (when the setup is done): "ஆனா இது பாதி கதை தான்." · "ஆனா இங்க தான் ஒரு twist." · "ஆனா இதுல ஒரு problem இருக்கு: [problem]."
   - **Noticing question** (right after a visual or a number; answer it in the next line): "இதுல ஒரு விஷயம் கவனிச்சீங்களா? [observation]." · "இதுல weird-ஆன விஷயம் என்ன தெரியுமா? [twist]." · "இந்த [number] பாத்தீங்களா? ஏன்னு யோசிங்க."
   - **Reminder** (what they are waiting for): "சரி, அப்போ [hook question]?" · "இப்போ [A] தெரியும். ஆனா [B] ஏன்?"
   - **Stakes** only when a source supports them, and never as fear on a health topic. Default: don't use.
29. **After the payoff, only the twist line.** No recap, no "அதாவது…", no bonus tip; the recap is where the graph drops (rule 8). Delay with a question or a real step, never with filler words. A "here's the best part" line belongs before the payoff as a rehook, not after it.

**Checking a hook before showing it to Vasanth**
30. **Four ways a hook fails** (Hook Master, after Kallaway). Check every hook option against all four, and fix them in this order:
    1. **Delay:** the topic is not clear in the first line. Throat-clearing such as "வணக்கம் friends", "இன்னைக்கு நாம பாக்க போறது…" or a channel intro.
    2. **Confusion:** the viewer can't say in a few words what the video is about.
    3. **Irrelevance:** the viewer doesn't see why it is about them (no Tamil home, body or street they recognise).
    4. **Disinterest:** there is no question the viewer wants answered.
    Then a quick panel: would a fast scroller, a sceptic, a beginner, an expert (doctor or historian), an elder in the family and a rival creator each stay or swipe, and why? Show Vasanth only hooks that pass.
31. **Hook formats that fit a faceless Tamil explainer** (Hook Master names; examples ours):
    - **Investigator:** "உங்க [thing] ஏன் [problem]? காரணம் நீங்க நினைக்குறது இல்ல." This is the channel's own "அது ஏன்?" shape.
    - **Fortune teller:** "அடுத்த தடவை [situation], [what will be different]." Ep 01's ending uses it; it also works as a hook.
    - **Contrarian:** "[common habit] பண்ணாதீங்க." Only when health-authority guidance says so; safer on city and daily-life topics.
    - Not for us: Teacher ("how to X without Y") is advice, which we avoid on health; Experimenter needs a creator on camera (Mani can stand in only for harmless tests).
32. **Words that make viewers swipe or that invent authority.** Never use: "யாருமே சொல்ல மாட்டாங்க" (nobody tells you), "ரகசியம்" (secret), "scientists சொல்றாங்க" without naming who, "எல்லாரும்…" claims with no source, round numbers with no source, "100 %", "guaranteed", "miracle", and health promises such as "வலியே இருக்காது" or "safe". *Grade: PRACTITIONER; the no-invented-authority part matches our sourcing rule.*

**Story formats**
33. **Kallaway's ten story formats are all first-person creator stories.** Most need a face (About me, Day in the life, Win, Personal update, Challenge, Goal journey): skip them. Three fit a faceless narrator:
    - **Lesson from others:** tell a real, named person's story (the engineer who planned a town, the doctor who found a cause). Make them a vivid character in a specific situation, and land the lesson as a moment or a quote. **The quote must come from a credible source, never invented.** Good for the city series.
    - **Before and after:** the size of the gap is the whole effect; the switch lands on a beat; 15–30 s is enough. Good for city "then vs now" with an old map against today.
    - **Failed attempts (from the hero's journey):** before the explanation, Mani tries the common wrong fixes (stamping the leg, rubbing it). This doubles as the exaggeration joke. Show the myth once only (rule 17).

## Checklist for every script

- One question in the hook, and nothing in the video that doesn't answer it. Save a second great hook for its own episode.
- The visual, spoken and on-screen text hooks match, and the hook is no longer than about 3 s.
- A foreshadow line within 3 s that names the conflict.
- Every line is a "but" or a "therefore". If a line is an "and then", cut it.
- A twist on the expected answer. The last line is the high point, and the video stops right after it.
- Read it aloud: plain spoken Tamil, short words. Cut every word that isn't needed.
- Optional and untested: a loop line, only if it adds no time and doesn't blur the payoff. The video cuts within ~0.5 s of the payoff.
- The hook passes the mute test: frame 0 plus the on-screen text alone name the subject.
- Belief → true reality → one quick proof within ~5 s (rules 14–15).
- The key fact said twice: once plainly, once with the Tamil analogy. The myth is said only once (rule 17).
- One target emotion named; no manufactured fear (rule 21).
- Sentence lengths vary: mostly short, a few medium, no run-ons (rule 22).
- Walkaway test: the viewer can repeat the fact in one sentence to their family (rule 19).
- The answer line is marked and sits in the last third; a question, not the answer, follows the hook (rule 27).
- One rehook at about 40–55 % that names this video's next thing (rule 28).
- Nothing after the payoff except the twist line: no recap, no bonus tip, no filler (rule 29).
- Every hook option passes the four-failure check and uses none of the banned words (rules 30, 32).
