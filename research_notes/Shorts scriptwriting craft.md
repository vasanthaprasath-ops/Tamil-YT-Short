# Shorts scriptwriting craft: what proven creators do (2026-11-13)

Most of these sources are secondary write-ups: interview summaries and creator blogs. Treat the rules as practitioner craft, not measured science. They agree with each other, and with our psychology notes (`Short video formats that work/psychology_attention_sharing.md`).

Evidence grades for these rules are in `reports/Short form video craft playbook.md`. All rules below are PRACTITIONER level. Rules 8 and 10 have creator numbers behind them; none has a controlled test.

## Rules

1. **One subject, one question.** A hook names one topic and puts the same question in every viewer's head. If the hook is vague, each viewer guesses a different subject and leaves. Kallaway, via [SozAI transcript](https://sozai.app/transcript/create-killer-hook-impossible-skip/) and [Recapio](https://recapio.com/digest/how-to-create-a-killer-hook-impossible-to-skip-by-kallaway).
2. **The three hooks must match.** What's on screen, what's said and the on-screen text all point at the same thing. Final audit: is the subject clear? Is the question clear? Are all three aligned? Would I watch the second sentence? Kallaway (same sources).
3. **The hook is under 3 seconds, works on mute, and uses very simple words.** Jenny Hoyos checks her scripts with a readability tool and aims for a 5th-grade reading level or below. Her test: would this work as a long-form title and thumbnail? [Creator Science podcast](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/). Readability tools are English-only. For Tamil, use spoken colloquial words and short clauses, and explain any technical term in one breath.
4. **Foreshadow right after the hook.** Within about 3 s, say what's coming, what the conflict is and what the viewer gets by the end. [Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/); [Marketing Examined](https://marketing-examined.beehiiv.com/p/jenny-hoyos-shortform-video-playbook).
5. **Give the viewer a visible mechanism.** For example, three clues or three steps, so they know how far they are from the payoff. Hoyos (same).
6. **Build the body from "but" and "therefore", never "and then".** Every beat changes the situation. This is Trey Parker and Matt Stone's rule, which Hoyos and Kallaway use for Shorts. [Matt Rickard](https://cdn.matt-rickard.com/but-therefore); [Perell](https://perell.com/note/but-therefore-rule/).
7. **Meet the expectation, then add a twist.** End on the biggest emotion: leave viewers "satisfied, but surprised". Hoyos, [Marketing Examined](https://marketing-examined.beehiiv.com/p/jenny-hoyos-shortform-video-playbook).
8. **End the moment the promise is delivered.** Zack D. Films' "How Stitches Work" (31 s, 253M views) asks one question and stops when it answers it. Hoyos: "Whatever you say you're gonna do, you end it right after you do it." Cutting one second from an ending raised her retention from 83% to 88%. [Rusnok breakdown](https://danielrusnok.substack.com/p/how-a-faceless-channel-engineered); [Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/).
9. **Write the hook and the last line first.** Then fill in the middle. Hoyos (same).
10. **Length.** Hoyos' best Shorts average about 34 s, and she found 10–20 s worked better on TikTok. Galloway's study of 5,400 Shorts found a clear link between length and views: Shorts with average view duration above 50 s averaged 4.1M views. That is correlational, from established channels in the 60-second era. Default to 30–45 s and go longer only when retention stays flat. [Thread Reader](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1646898356419981315.html); [Creator Science](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/jenny-hoyos/).
11. **Set the right expectation in the first seconds** and re-engage the viewer regularly. This comes from the reported MrBeast production guide, whose authenticity is unconfirmed. [Creator Handbook](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/leaked-document-allegedly-reveals-mrbeasts-secrets-to-youtube-success-the-key-takeaways/). This guide is about long-form and its authenticity is unconfirmed. Ignore any 're-hook every N seconds' number. The rule is that every line changes the situation.
12. **Captions are part of the script.** Break lines for emphasis and highlight keywords. [Toptal](https://www.toptal.com/creator/post/how-to-write-a-youtube-script). (Practitioner opinion. Show 2–4-word phrases timed to speech; keyword highlighting is untested.)
13. **No spoken subscribe ask by default.** Spoken calls to action show as retention dips, and Zack D. Films uses none. Earn the follow with the recurring stamp, Paati and the series name.

## Checklist for every script

- One question in the hook, and nothing in the video that doesn't answer it. Save a second great hook for its own episode.
- The visual, spoken and on-screen text hooks match, and the hook is no longer than about 3 s.
- A foreshadow line within 3 s that names the conflict.
- Every line is a "but" or a "therefore". If a line is an "and then", cut it.
- A twist on the expected answer. The last line is the high point, and the video stops right after it.
- Read it aloud: plain spoken Tamil, short words. Cut every word that isn't needed.
- Optional and untested: a loop line, only if it adds no time and doesn't blur the payoff. The video cuts within ~0.5 s of the payoff.
- The hook passes the mute test: frame 0 plus the on-screen text alone name the subject.
