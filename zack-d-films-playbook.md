## 3. The mechanism (what is load-bearing, not surface)

Counterfactual test — remove each thing, does it still pop?

- **Remove the 3D animation** → weaker, but a strong topic still works as plain explainer. *Animation is an amplifier, not the driver.*
- **Remove the topic's self-relevance** (e.g., stitches on a stranger's machine instead of skin) → collapses. *Driver #1.*
- **Remove the visual of the thing happening** (narration over text) → collapses; the wince/curiosity needs to be *seen*. *Driver #2.*
- **Remove the emoji in the title** → minor. Incidental-ish (it does set the emotion: 😨 fear, 🤔 curiosity, 😮 surprise).

**One-line principle:** *Pick something the viewer has on their body or in their daily life, show it happening in the first second, and answer a question they never thought to ask.*

**Share trigger:** "Bro look at this" — the gross/fear reaction is social (people send it to watch the other person wince), plus the fact is reusable in conversation.

## 4. Title formula (his exact patterns)

- `How [familiar thing] Works` — How Stitches Work
- `Why [familiar thing] [odd fact]` — Why Bees Die After They Sting You
- `What Is [thing on your body]?` — What Is A Skin Tag?
- `Could / Can [surprising question]?` — Can Seeds Really Sprout Inside You?
- `[Situation you could be in]` — Stuck In A Water Slide
- Ends with one emoji that names the emotion.

Titles are 3–7 words, plain words, no clickbait caps (except an occasional EXPLAINED).

## 5. Video structure (from general knowledge of the format — verify on 5 of his shorts)

Not verified frame-by-frame in this session. Watch 5 top shorts and check each point:

1. **0–1s:** the problem is already on screen (finger with splinter, man in trunk). No intro, no "hi guys".
2. **1–3s:** one spoken line that restates the question.
3. **Middle:** step-by-step visual explanation, each step a new shot every ~1.5–3s.
4. **Twist / payoff:** the surprising answer or a gross close-up.
5. **End:** stops abruptly, no outro, so the loop restarts cleanly (replays count).
6. Length ~30–60s. Calm, plain narrator voice. Captions on screen.

## 6. Replicability check (be honest)

- **Account size:** NOT replicable. 29.6M subs gives every new short a 3M+ floor. A new channel starts at a few hundred views.
- **Volume:** he has 4.1K videos. Hits are a small % of output — survivorship. Plan for many misses.
- **Replicable:** the topic selection logic and the "show it in second one" hook. Those transfer to any language.
- **Not replicable:** his style itself. Copying his look/name/characters = derivative + YouTube "reused/inauthentic content" risk. Take the mechanism, build your own look.

## 7. Applied to a Tamil audience

### Evaluating the initial ideas

| Idea | Verdict |
|---|---|
| City-targeted ("Namma Madurai la…", "Chennai la…") | Strong identity hook and share trigger ("tag your Madurai friend"), but each video only fully lands with one city. Use as a **recurring series inside the channel**, not the whole channel. |
| God / temples | Huge interest, but saturated ("kovil ragasiyam" channels everywhere), misinformation-prone, and religiously sensitive. Use only as **"the science/engineering behind"** — never mock belief, check facts. |
| Tamil + random interesting | Too vague as a niche. Needs a sharper frame. |

### Recommended core concept: "Paati sonnadhu unmaiya?" (elders' rules, explained)

Every Tamil household grew up with the same rules. That is the Tamil version of Zack's "your own body / your daily life" driver — maximum self-relevance, plus the family-WhatsApp share trigger and comment debates.

Example episodes:
- North pakkam thalai vechu thoonga koodadhu — why? (magnetic field claim, what science says)
- Kaayathula manjal podradhu — does turmeric really heal wounds?
- Saapta udane kulikka koodadhu — why?
- Kaal maraththu pochu — what is actually happening in your leg?
- Kolam arisi maavula podradhu — why rice flour?
- Thengai odaikradhu, metti, thali — the reason behind (respectful, factual)
- Saapadu ilai la saapdradhu — any real benefit?

### Secondary buckets (same Zack mechanism, Tamil daily life)

- **Body:** why ears pop on Kodaikanal ghat road, why eyes water cutting onion, what happens when you swallow a tamarind seed.
- **Everyday object mystery:** why autos have that meter sound, why bus tickets are that size, why Chennai buildings have water tanks on top, why idli plates have holes.
- **Survival:** what to do if stuck in a flooded car (Chennai floods — very high relevance), if bitten by a snake in a village, if a lift stops.
- **City series:** "Madurai la mattum yen…" / "Chennai la yen…" — one per week, rotate cities.
- **Weird Tamil history:** why the Big Temple's shadow claim is (or isn't) true, why Pondy streets are in a grid, why Chettinad houses have those pillars.

## 8. "AI-made but not slop" rules

YouTube demonetizes mass-produced, repetitive, low-effort content. What separates you from slop:

1. **Researched script** — one real, checkable fact per video. Wrong facts kill trust (and comments will catch it).
2. **Your own Tamil voice** — Tamil TTS still sounds robotic. Own voice = authenticity + avoids "inauthentic content" flags.
3. **One recurring character** (consistent face/outfit across videos) — viewers recognize it, it becomes the brand.
4. **Visual of the thing happening in second one** — AI image/video tools are good at this if prompted shot-by-shot.
5. **No templated sameness** — vary shots; don't reuse the same 5 clips.

Pipeline: script → shot list (6–12 shots) → images with a consistent character (Flux / Nano Banana / Ideogram) → image-to-video (Kling / Veo / Runway) → own voiceover → edit + captions (CapCut).

## 9. Money — set expectations

- YPP for Shorts: 1,000 subs + 10M Shorts views in 90 days.
- Indian Shorts revenue per 1,000 views is very low. Tamil Shorts alone pay little; money comes from volume, brand deals, and later reusing the same visuals for an English (or Hindi) version, which pays more.
- The animation is the expensive part; voice is cheap. Plan from day one to make each video in Tamil first, then dub.

### The differentiator: an honest verdict stamp

Every episode ends on one of three stamps: **UNMAI ✅ / PAADHI UNMAI ⚠️ / POI ❌**.

- Existing channels say "ancestors were always right". We give a real verdict, which builds trust.
- "Paadhi unmai" will be the most common answer, and it starts the most comment debates.
- Never mock the elders. Frame it as "here's what they noticed, here's what science says now".

## 11. First 30 days

1. Lock the concept (Section 7).
2. Write 30 titles using the Section 4 formula.
3. Make 3 pilot shorts. Judge them yourself against Section 5 before posting.
4. Post 1 per day for 30 days. Same time daily.
5. After each, check YouTube Studio: **"Viewed vs swiped away"** %. Above ~70% viewed = hook works. Low = fix first second.
6. After 30 days, double down on the bucket with the best viewed %, drop the worst.
